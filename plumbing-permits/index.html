What counts as a plumbing permit here

A permit lands on this page when it is a plumbing permit or its scope names plumbing work — a water heater, a re-pipe, a sewer line, a gas line, a boiler. It is the biggest of the five trades on this site by permit count, and the steadiest: the monthly counts below barely move with the seasons.

Two speeds: emergencies and new builds

Water-heater and re-pipe permits are pulled at the job. A failed water heater does not wait a week, so those permits are this week's work, and newest-first is the only way to read them. Sewer work is close behind — it usually starts with something going wrong underground.

Plumbing inside new construction runs at building speed instead: filed with the project, roughed in months before anyone turns on a tap. A rising new-construction count is next season's plumbing work announcing itself early.

Questions, answered from the data

How many plumbing permits were issued in the last 12 months?

116,698 plumbing permits were issued across the 20 counties PermitsByCounty covers in the last 12 months. Bexar County issued the most, with 19,826. Each county report carries its own coverage dates and source.

Which plumbing permits mean work right now?

Water-heater and re-pipe permits. Both are pulled when the job is done or about to start — a failed water heater does not wait. New-construction plumbing is the opposite: it is filed with the build and runs months ahead.

How steady is plumbing work across the year?

Steadier than any other trade here: monthly plumbing permits across the covered counties ran between 4,088 and 10,056 over the last year — no slow season in the record.

Does a plumbing permit name the plumber?

Where the source publishes a contractor of record, the county report carries the business name. Homeowner names are never published on this site — public business and permit facts only.
