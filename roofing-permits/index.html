What counts as a roofing permit here

Our classifier reads each permit's type and scope text. A permit lands on this page when it is a re-roof or a roof replacement, or when the description names roofing work — shingles, a tear-off, a roof on a new build. A general remodel that never mentions the roof stays out, even if a roofer ends up working on the job. That one rule keeps the count honest, and it is why reading the description still matters before you treat any single permit as a job.

A roofing permit means the work is close

A re-roof permit is usually pulled days before the job starts, not months. A standard roof installs in one to three days, and the whole job often runs one to four weeks — so the newest permits are the live ones, and a re-roof permit from last month may already be finished and gone.

Roofing inside new construction runs the other way. The roof goes on long before anyone moves in, so those permits are a map of work coming over the next few months, not work you can knock on today. Storm work sits on top of both: after hard weather, watch for the count to jump in the county that got hit.

Questions, answered from the data

How many roofing permits were issued in the last 12 months?

49,660 roofing permits were issued across the 21 counties PermitsByCounty covers in the last 12 months. Los Angeles County issued the most, with 14,441. Each county report carries its own coverage dates and source.

What counts as a roofing job in a permit record?

A direct re-roof first, then a new build, then an addition or remodel where the description names roof work. Read the description every time — a general alteration permit with no roof scope is not a roofing job.

How new should a roofing permit be before I work it?

Work the newest first, and work them weekly. A standard roof installs in one to three days and the full job often runs one to four weeks, so a month-old re-roof permit may already be finished. Check the status before you drive.

Does a permit give me the homeowner's phone number?

Usually no. A permit normally carries the address, the work, the dates, and the contractor of record — not a verified homeowner phone number. If a seller promises one on every row, ask which record it came from.
