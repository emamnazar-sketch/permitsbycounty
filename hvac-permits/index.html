What counts as an HVAC permit here

A permit lands on this page when it is a mechanical permit or its scope names heating and cooling work — a furnace, an air conditioner, ductwork, a condensing unit. If you scan a county report by hand, “mechanical” is the word to look for: most cities file HVAC work under that name, not under HVAC.

HVAC permits run on the weather

A changeout permit is pulled when the new system goes in, so the work is happening that week — newest first is the only way to read this trade. Volume follows the weather, and the busiest month in the last year was June 2026 with 7,660 HVAC permits across the covered counties.

Mechanical permits inside new construction are the slow lane. They are filed with the project and run months ahead of move-in, so a building boom shows up here long before the systems are ever switched on.

Questions, answered from the data

How many hvac permits were issued in the last 12 months?

81,846 hvac permits were issued across the 20 counties PermitsByCounty covers in the last 12 months. Tarrant County issued the most, with 12,274. Each county report carries its own coverage dates and source.

Why do HVAC permits say “mechanical”?

Most cities file heating and cooling work as mechanical permits — it is the permit family that covers furnaces, air conditioners, and ductwork. When you scan a county report for HVAC work, look for the mechanical count.

How fast does an HVAC permit turn into work?

A changeout permit is pulled when the new system goes in, so the work is happening that week. Mechanical permits inside a new build are different: they are filed with the project and run months ahead of move-in.
