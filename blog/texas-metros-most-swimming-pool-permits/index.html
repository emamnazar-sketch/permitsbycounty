Which Texas metros have the most swimming-pool permits right now?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 4, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

Statewide, Harris County — Houston — has the most swimming-pool permits in Texas: HBWeekly counted 88 new pool permits there in September 2026, worth about $7.19 million. Among the Texas metros PermitsByCounty counts from its own live records, Travis County — Austin — leads right now, with 103 pool building permits issued in October 2026. Bexar County — San Antonio — counted 24 dedicated pool permits that month, and the City of Fort Worth filed 22. Each city files pools a different way, so read the counting notes under each city before you compare the numbers.

Pool permits in Texas, counted two honest ways

There are two fair ways to answer this question, and this post uses both, labeled every time.

The first is a statewide count from HBWeekly, the paid Texas permit service that has tracked this market since 1992. Its September 2026 review counted 427 new swimming-pool permits across Texas, worth about $31.4 million. That is the newest full statewide month we could check on November 4, 2026.

The second is our own count. PermitsByCounty pulls the Austin, San Antonio, and Fort Worth permit feeds every day, and all three passed their nightly checks on November 4, 2026. October 2026 is the newest full month in those records, so October is the month we rank here.

The two counts won’t match city for city, and they shouldn’t. HBWeekly counts new residential pool permits its own way. We count what each city actually files, under that city’s own permit types. Where the two disagree, we say so and show both.

Austin and Travis County: the busiest metro we count live

Austin issued 103 building permits for pools in October 2026. That is up from 75 in September, and it makes Travis County the busiest Texas metro in our own records right now.

Almost all of it is backyard work. Of the 103 October permits, 97 were residential and 6 were commercial. Over the last 12 months, Austin issued 1,076 pool building permits — a steady line of work, not one good month.

Two names pulled the most pool building permits in October. Cody Pools and Paradisa Homes each show 11. Aquamarine Pools of Texas shows 5. Cody Pools also led Austin in HBWeekly’s September count, with 8 permits — the same name on top in both counts, a month apart.

The work clusters in a few ZIP codes. ZIP 78704 led October with 20 pool building permits, then 78703 with 14 and 78757 with 12. A pool crew that works south and central Austin is standing where most of the new permits are.

One counting note, and it matters. Austin files one pool job as several permits — a building permit, then separate electrical and plumbing permits that also name the pool. In October, 232 Austin permits of all types mentioned a pool. We count only the 103 building permits, one per pool project, so the number stays honest. And we publish no Austin dollar total: most Austin pool building permits state a value of $0 or $1, so a total would be made-up precision. Counts only.

San Antonio and Bexar County: a smaller, cleaner count

San Antonio counted 24 pool permits in October 2026. The city makes this count easy: it issues a dedicated swimming-pool permit type, so a pool is one permit with pool in its name — no digging through descriptions.

The month before, September, the same count was 35. HBWeekly’s September count for Bexar County was 20 new residential pool permits, worth about $1.36 million. The gap between 35 and 20 is the method, not an error: the city’s pool permit types cover commercial pools and spas too, and HBWeekly counts new residential pools only. Both numbers are real. They answer slightly different questions.

Over the last 12 months, San Antonio issued 300 permits under its pool types. October’s work spread across the north side — ZIP 78260 shows 3 pool permits, and 78261, 78258, 78248, and 78247 show 2 each. No single ZIP runs away with it the way 78704 does in Austin.

San Antonio is the smallest of the big Texas pool metros in both counts. It’s still steady work: about two dozen to three dozen pool permits a month, every month, in one county.

Fort Worth: fewer pools, real dollar figures

Fort Worth filed 22 permits naming a pool in October 2026, and every one of them was residential. September was a little busier, with 31.

Fort Worth doesn’t issue a pool permit type. A pool shows up inside a residential building or accessory permit, with the pool named in the description — an in-ground pool, a pool and spa, a gunite pool. Fort Worth also dates its records by the application, not the issuance, so we count each pool permit once, in the month it was filed, and only when its status shows it was issued. That is the same counting rule we used for Fort Worth permits in our Fort Worth guide.

What Fort Worth gives back is honest money. Its pool permits carry real stated values: a median of $65,000 in October 2026, and $1,465,290 across the month’s 22 permits. Over the last 12 months, Fort Worth filed 429 pool permits with $61,789,136 in stated value, and the median held at $65,000. A Fort Worth pool, on paper, is a mid-sized backyard build — not the cheapest work in Texas, and not the luxury end either.

October’s Fort Worth pools sat in ZIP 76126 with 4, then 76244 and 76109 with 3 each. One caution that applies to this whole post: Tarrant County itself does not issue building permits. These are City of Fort Worth permits. Other Tarrant cities keep their own records, and their pools are not in this count.

The two metros missing from our count — and why

Houston should be at the top of this list, and in HBWeekly’s count it is: Harris County’s 88 pool permits in September 2026 beat every other Texas county. But PermitsByCounty publishes no Harris County pool count, because the Harris feed is blocked to us. Rather than dress up someone else’s number as ours, we show HBWeekly’s figure labeled as HBWeekly’s, and leave our own Harris row empty until the feed opens.

Dallas is the harder gap. The city’s open-data permit feed stopped refreshing in 2024 — the local lead service Tradewind leaves Dallas out of its coverage for exactly that reason, checked November 3, 2026. HBWeekly still counts the Dallas metro from its own sources, but PermitsByCounty has no live Dallas record to count, so Dallas gets no number here either. A list that skips two big metros on purpose, and says why, is worth more than a complete-looking list built on stale records.

So the honest ranking reads like this. Statewide, Harris County leads. Of the metros with live, checkable records that anyone can pull today, Austin leads by a wide margin, San Antonio and Fort Worth run smaller and steadier, and Dallas can’t be ranked from public records right now.

What one pool permit sets in motion

A pool permit isn’t one job. It’s a short parade of them, in a set order.

Excavation comes first, then plumbing and steel, then the shell, then electrical, then decking and coping, then plaster and fill, and a fence or barrier that passes its own check before the pool can be used. Each stage is a different crew, and most of those crews are not the pool builder’s own staff.

The timing helps the crews who watch permits. Building permits are filed 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks, per Shovels’ supplier guide, checked November 4, 2026. A pool permit issued this week is excavation work this month and decking work a few weeks after that. If you sell excavation, gunite, decking, fencing, or pool equipment, the permit list is your order book, a month early.

That is also why the metro ranking matters beyond pool builders. A metro issuing 100 pool permits a month is issuing 100 of everything a pool needs. A metro issuing two dozen is a smaller, tighter market where one relationship with a busy builder carries further.

How to work this list, step by step

Step 1: Pick your metro by the count and the method. If you run crews, Austin’s volume is the draw. If you want stated values before you drive out, Fort Worth’s records carry them. Read each city’s counting note before you trust its number.

Step 2: Pull the newest month first. Work October’s pool permits before September’s. A pool permitted last month is breaking ground now; a pool permitted last spring is already plastered.

Step 3: Sort by ZIP, not just by date. Austin’s October pools cluster in 78704, 78703, and 78757. A tight map means one crew can cover more bids in a day.

Step 4: Note the builder on each permit. In Austin, two names pulled 11 pool permits each in October. Builders who pull pools every month are the accounts worth a real introduction, not a cold flyer.

Step 5: Match your call to your stage. Excavation and plumbing call in the first days after the permit. Decking, fencing, and equipment calls land a few weeks later. The permit date tells you which week your work starts.

Step 6: Check again next month. Pool counts move. One month is a snapshot; three months in a row is a market. We update this ranking when the new month’s records land.

Mistakes that make this ranking lie

Don’t compare the three city counts as if they were counted the same way. Austin’s 103 counts building permits only. San Antonio’s 24 counts a dedicated permit type. Fort Worth’s 22 counts permits that name a pool in the description. The ranking holds — Austin is busiest by any of the three methods — but the exact gaps are method, not just market.

Don’t quote an Austin pool dollar total. There is none worth quoting. Most Austin pool building permits state $0 or $1 as the value, so any Austin total is arithmetic on placeholder numbers. Fort Worth’s values are the real ones in this post.

Don’t treat October as the year’s pace. October is one month, and pool work is seasonal. The steadier read is the trailing year: 1,076 pool building permits in Austin, 429 pool permits filed in Fort Worth, 300 pool permits in San Antonio.

Don’t go looking for a PermitsByCounty Harris or Dallas pool list. There isn’t one, for the reasons above. Anyone who shows you a fresh Dallas pool list from the city’s open data is showing you old records with a new date on them.

Frequently asked questions

Which Texas metro has the most swimming-pool permits right now?

Statewide, Harris County — Houston — leads, with 88 new pool permits in HBWeekly’s September 2026 count. Among the metros PermitsByCounty counts from live records, Travis County — Austin — leads, with 103 pool building permits issued in October 2026.

How many pool permits did Austin issue in October 2026?

The City of Austin issued 103 building permits for pools in October 2026 — 97 residential and 6 commercial. Counting every permit type that mentions a pool, including the separate electrical and plumbing permits for the same jobs, the total is 232, which is why we count building permits only.

Why is there no Houston or Dallas number from PermitsByCounty?

What is a typical Fort Worth pool worth on its permit?

In October 2026, the median stated value on a Fort Worth pool permit was $65,000, and the same median held across the last 12 months. That’s the value the filer declared on the permit, not a bid or a final price.

When should a sub or supplier call about a pool permit?

Soon after it issues. Permits are filed 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks. Early-stage trades — excavation, plumbing, electrical — belong in the first days. Decking, fencing, and equipment fit a few weeks later, while the shell is going in.
