How much does a building permit cost in Los Angeles?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 19, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

The building permit cost Los Angeles charges depends on the value of your work, not on a flat price. Los Angeles prices a one- or two-family building permit from Table 1-A of the Municipal Code, then adds plan check and a stack of small surcharges. A $12,000 re-roof, filed as an express permit with no plan check, costs $283.95 all-in. A $25,000 kitchen remodel with electrical and plumbing work costs $629.67. A $25,000 building project with plan check costs $1,353.93.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • There is no single Los Angeles permit price. The city starts with the value of the work and works from Table 1-A.
  • Small express jobs skip plan check: a $12,000 re-roof is $283.95, an $8,000 window job is $220.88.
  • Remodels take one combined permit: a $25,000 kitchen with electrical and plumbing is $629.67, a $15,000 bath is $492.44.
  • Bigger work adds plan check at 90% of the permit fee: a $25,000 project with plan check is $1,353.93, and a 600 sq ft detached ADU is $4,512.29.
  • City of Los Angeles and Los Angeles County are two different offices with two different fee tables. Check which one covers your address first.

Start with which Los Angeles you mean

This trips up a lot of people. A Los Angeles mailing address can sit inside the City of Los Angeles, inside another city, or in unincorporated county land. Each place runs its own permit office and its own fees.

The City of Los Angeles office is the Department of Building and Safety, called LADBS for short. It issues permits inside city limits, through its ePlanLA portal and its services portal. Most of the numbers in this post are LADBS numbers, because most readers asking this question mean the city.

Los Angeles County Public Works handles building permits in unincorporated areas. The county uses its own Table 1-A, its own issuing fee, and its own plan check rate. The county numbers sit near the end of this post so you can see the split in one place. Do not pay a city fee estimate on a county job, or the other way round. Both offices will send you to the right desk, but you will lose days.

If you are not sure which one covers a house, search the address in each office’s permit portal. The one that returns the property record is usually the right office. You can also look at a past permit for the same address. The office name on that old permit is the office that still covers the property in most cases.

How LADBS builds the fee

LADBS starts with valuation. That is the total value of the work on the permit: labor, materials, finish work, and the trade work that goes with it. You write a value on the application. The department can revise it if it looks too low for the scope you described.

The base fee comes from Table 1-A of the Los Angeles Municipal Code. PermitCalculator, which rebuilt the table from LADBS permit cards and checked it on September 27, 2026, puts the printed table this way: $40 plus $1.25 per $100 of value from $2,000 to $20,000, and $170 plus $6 per $1,000 from $20,000 to $50,000. Every started $100 or $1,000 counts in full, so a value just over a line is priced as if it hit the next step.

Then the surcharges land. On top of the table fee, LADBS bills a state Strong Motion fee of 0.013% of value, a $27 permit issuing fee, a 3% Development Services Center surcharge, a 6% Systems surcharge, a 6% Planning surcharge, a 10% Planning General Plan Maintenance surcharge, a $10 Planning fee, and a state Building Standards fee of $1 per $25,000 of value. None of these lines is big on its own. Together they add close to $94 on a $12,000 re-roof, on top of a $190 table fee.

That stack is why two quotes for the same job can look far apart. One contractor lists the table fee only. The other lists the full bill. Ask for the permit line itemized, with each surcharge named, before you sign. You want the second kind of quote.

Building permit cost Los Angeles: common projects

Here are the billed totals PermitCalculator read from LADBS permit cards. Use them as a starting point for your own job, then run your real value through the LADBS fee estimator or ask the counter for the current bill. Fees are indexed and can move.

A $12,000 re-roof is $283.95 as an express permit. A $25,000 re-roof on the same express route is $448.30. Window work at $8,000 is $220.88, and siding at $10,000 is $252.42. These four jobs share one trait: they are like-for-like work on the outside of a house, so LADBS can issue them as express permits with no plan check. That is what keeps them cheap.

Remodels cost more because the trades ride along. A kitchen or bath remodel on a one- or two-family home takes one combined building and mechanical permit. The code adds 26% of the table fee for electrical work, 26% for plumbing, and 13% for mechanical work. A $25,000 kitchen with electrical and plumbing work comes to $629.67. A $15,000 bathroom that also swaps its exhaust fan comes to $492.44.

Trade-only permits are the cheapest line on the page. They carry a $55 minimum for one item. An EV charger circuit is $59.95 with surcharges. A water heater with its expansion tank counts as two items and costs $98.10. An electrical panel permit is listed at $59.95, solar panel work at $215.82, and an HVAC replacement at $158.05.

When plan check joins the bill

Plan check is the city’s review of your drawings. Express permits skip it. Most other work pays it.

For city building permits, plan check is 90% of the permit fee. That near-double step is the main reason a plan-check job costs so much more than an express job of the same value. A building permit for a $25,000 project is listed at $1,353.93 with plan check and surcharges. The same value filed as a $12,000 project with plan check is $816.06, and an $8,000 project is $609.53.

A new deck filed as a plan-check alteration comes to $319.35. A 600 sq ft detached ADU, with permit and plan check together, comes to $4,512.29. An ADU also brings costs this post does not fold into that figure: school fees and utility charges are billed apart from LADBS. One current source for those extra lines is the Los Angeles Unified school fee, listed at $5.38 per sq ft from August 11, 2026 in a fee-method guide checked for this post. On an ADU over 500 sq ft, that one line can add thousands, so ask about it before you lock a budget.

PermitPlace, a permit service that files in Los Angeles, describes the same shape from the process side: regular plan check for larger work takes 4 to 8 weeks for a first review, while small residential jobs can qualify for counter plan check, sometimes the same day. Time is not a fee, but a slow review can cost you a contractor’s slot. Price both when you plan.

Why the online estimator runs high

If you run the LADBS online Fee Estimate System first, your number may come back higher than the bill you would actually pay on an express permit. PermitCalculator flags this plainly: the estimator uses a higher, inflation-adjusted table and adds a Green Building fee, while express permits are billed on the printed table.

The re-roof shows the gap in one line. The estimator puts a $12,000 re-roof at $435.53. Eight $12,000 re-roof permits issued in 2026 were each billed $283.95. The permit card is the record of what was charged, so this post uses the billed figures for express work and says so each time.

Use the estimator the way the city means it: as a budget ceiling while you plan. Then ask for the itemized bill at filing. If the two numbers differ, the itemized bill is the one you pay.

What does not need a permit

Some work is exempt, and paying for a permit you do not need helps no one. PermitCalculator’s Los Angeles page, reading LAMC 91.106.2, lists paint, cosmetic updates, and fixture swaps as no-permit work. Since December 31, 2025, that section also covers work valued at $2,500 or less, unless it affects the structure, public safety, a department order, or a change of use.

The same page lists more exempt lines: masonry or concrete fences up to 3 1/2 feet and other fences up to 10 feet, except pool barriers; platforms, walks, and driveways no more than 30 inches above grade and not over a basement; and pools no deeper than 24 inches with no more than 250 sq ft of surface. Decks over 30 inches, re-roofs, siding, window replacement, and remodels need a permit. Electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work has its own rules, so a job that is exempt as building work can still need a trade permit.

Read the exempt list with care. It is a city list. County land and other cities inside Los Angeles County run their own lists, and a homeowners association can add its own approval on top of either one.

The county table, for unincorporated addresses

If your address is in unincorporated Los Angeles County, Public Works runs your permit, and the math changes.

The county code adds a permit issuance fee of $39.99 on top of its Table 1-A fee. Its Table 1-A, with fee changes effective July 1, 2025, starts at $63.21 for a value up to $700, then $94.87 plus $24.44 for each additional $1,000 or part of it over $1,000, up to $25,000. At $25,001 to $50,000 the base is $682.83, and at $50,001 to $100,000 it is $1,159.71. Over $100,000, the base is $1,867.20 plus $9.66 for each additional $1,000.

County plan check is 85% of the building permit fee, with a minimum of $118.64. A combined county building permit is 1.60 times the Table 1-A fee, and a combined swimming pool permit is two times the fee plus a barrier inspection fee. Those multipliers are county rules. Do not carry the city’s 90% plan check or its combined-permit percentages into a county estimate.

How to get your exact number

Start with a real scope. Write down the work in plain words: what comes out, what goes in, and which trades touch it. A vague scope gives you a vague fee, and the counter will price the scope you describe, not the one in your head.

Next, put a fair value on that scope. Use your contractor’s bid or a written estimate. Do not lowball the value to shave the fee. The department can revise a value that does not fit the work, and a revised value restarts the fee math mid-review.

Then run the LADBS fee estimator if you are in the city, and keep the result as your ceiling. For an express job, expect the billed total to land closer to the permit-card figures in this post. For a plan-check job, budget the permit fee, the 90% plan check, and the surcharges together from the start.

Ask two questions at filing. First, is this one combined permit or separate trade permits? The answer changes the total. Second, what else is billed apart from LADBS? School fees, utility charges, and planning or fire review can sit outside the building permit bill, and each one has its own desk and its own due date.

Last, keep the permit card. It itemizes every charge you paid. If you sell the house later, that card and the final inspection sign-off are the cleanest proof that the work was permitted and closed.

Common mistakes

The first mistake is budgeting the table fee only. On small jobs the surcharges add about half again. On plan-check jobs they ride on top of a fee that has already almost doubled. Budget the full stack.

The second mistake is mixing up city and county. The tables, the issuing fees, and the plan check rates all differ. Five minutes with the address in both portals saves a wrong estimate and a wrong line.

The third mistake is treating the estimator as the bill. It runs high on express work by design of its table. Use it as a ceiling, not a quote.

The fourth mistake is forgetting the costs outside LADBS. An ADU or a new home can draw school fees and utility charges that dwarf a small remodel’s permit. Ask for the full list of desks that will bill your project before you file, not after.

The fifth mistake is starting work while you wait. A permit pulled after work starts is a different, harder conversation, and the city can make you open finished work for inspection. File first, even if the review takes weeks.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Los Angeles?

It depends on the value and type of work. A $12,000 re-roof filed as an express permit costs $283.95 all-in. A $25,000 kitchen remodel with electrical and plumbing work costs $629.67. A $25,000 project that needs plan check costs $1,353.93. Los Angeles prices the permit from Table 1-A, then adds plan check and surcharges.

How is the Los Angeles permit fee calculated?

LADBS takes the value of the work, finds the base fee in Table 1-A of the Municipal Code, adds plan check at 90% of the fee if your job needs review, then adds the issuing fee and surcharges: 3% Development Services Center, 6% Systems, 6% Planning, 10% General Plan Maintenance, a $10 Planning fee, the state Strong Motion fee, and the state Building Standards fee.

Why did the LADBS estimator give me a higher number?

The online Fee Estimate System uses a higher, inflation-adjusted table and adds a Green Building fee that express permits are not billed. On a $12,000 re-roof the estimator shows $435.53, while eight permits issued in 2026 at that value were each billed $283.95. Treat the estimator as a budget ceiling and the itemized permit card as the bill.

Do I need a permit for a kitchen remodel in Los Angeles?

Yes, in most cases where you move or add electrical, plumbing, gas, or structure. A Los Angeles kitchen remodel takes one combined building and mechanical permit, priced with add-ons of 26% of the table fee for electrical, 26% for plumbing, and 13% for mechanical work. Swapping cabinets in the same layout with no trade work is listed as cosmetic and exempt.

Is the fee different in Los Angeles County than in the city?

Yes. Unincorporated county addresses go through Los Angeles County Public Works, not LADBS. The county adds a $39.99 issuing fee, uses its own Table 1-A amounts effective July 1, 2025, and charges plan check at 85% of the permit fee with a $118.64 minimum. Check which office covers your address before you budget.

What other costs sit outside the building permit fee?

School fees, utility connection charges, and separate planning or fire review can all be billed apart from the LADBS permit. An ADU source checked for this post lists the Los Angeles Unified school fee at $5.38 per sq ft from August 11, 2026. Ask at filing for every desk that will bill your project, so nothing lands as a surprise.
