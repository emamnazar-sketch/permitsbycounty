How much does a building permit cost in Chicago, New York City, or San Francisco?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 3, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

For the same $25,000 home project, the city building permit alone costs $602 in Chicago, $227 in New York City for a one- to three-family home, and $1,429.18 in San Francisco, per city fee schedules checked November 2, 2026. Chicago charges a flat $602 minimum on plan-based permits. New York charges by job cost, starting low. San Francisco charges plan review plus issuance by value, so its bill climbs fastest — and trade permits are extra in all three cities.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Same $25,000 job, three bills: Chicago $602, New York City $227, San Francisco $1,429.18 — building permit only.
  • Chicago is flat at the bottom: an $8,000 job and a $25,000 job both pay the $602 plan-based minimum.
  • New York City is cheap on paper for a small house job, but most filings need a licensed architect or engineer, and that fee is separate.
  • San Francisco is the high one: plan review and issuance both rise with value, and the trade permits are separate.
  • Budget the city fee, the trade permits, and the drawings as three different lines.

Why the same job costs three different amounts

There is no national permit fee. Each city writes its own fee table, and these three cities picked three different ways to charge.

Chicago asks one flat minimum for plan-based work. If your job is small, the minimum is the fee. Bigger jobs climb by a formula based on square feet and scope, but small jobs never drop below the floor.

New York City asks for a slice of the job cost. For a one-, two-, or three-family home, the slice starts at $130 and rises slowly. The catch sits outside the fee table: most filings need a registered architect or licensed engineer, paid on top of the city fee.

San Francisco charges twice on value: a plan review fee, plus an issuance fee. Both rise with the value of the work. Then the trades bill you again, one permit at a time.

The side-by-side numbers: one job, three cities

Use the same job in each city, or the comparison means nothing. Here is a plain building permit — no trade permits yet — from each city’s fee schedule.

How much does a building permit cost in Chicago?

Chicago charges a $602 minimum for a plan-based building permit under its 2026 Amended Building Permit Fee Tables. Plan-based means the city reviews drawings first — most remodels, additions, and structural work file this way. An $8,000 project pays $602, and a $25,000 project also pays $602, because the formula result still sits at the floor. Bigger work climbs above it, by size, building type, and scope.

Two Chicago costs catch people off guard. First, the deposit. From January 6, 2026, a plan-based application starts with a $600 non-refundable deposit. If the city finds the application substantially incomplete, or filed under the wrong review program, it charges a penalty equal to the deposit — another $600. A sloppy filing can put $1,200 at risk before review starts. File complete, and file under the right program.

Deposit $600; penalty for a substantially incomplete or ineligible application $600

Second, not every Chicago permit is plan-based. Small, simple work can file through the Express Permit Program at flat fees well below $602 — a routine $15,000 bathroom inside 500 square feet, with no load-bearing work, is $325 on that route. Stand-alone scopes carry their own fees too — a roof repair with structural work is $900. From mid-March 2026, many fence fees went down; one tracked comparison puts that cut at $150 to $75.

One boundary matters. Chicago and Cook County are not the same permit office. Work in unincorporated Cook County files with the county, on its own e-permit list and fees. A Chicago number tells you nothing about a county job.

How much does a building permit cost in New York City?

For a one-, two-, or three-family home, New York City prices an alteration permit off the cost of the work. The rule sits in Table 28-112.2 of the Administrative Code: $130 for the first $5,000 of job cost, plus $2.60 for each added $1,000 or part of one. A $45 records-management fee goes on every alteration application for these homes.

The math is short. An $8,000 job: $130, plus three times $2.60, plus $45, is $182.80. A $12,000 job is $193.20. A $25,000 job is $227. Local Law 128 of 2024 set the current rates, in force since December 21, 2025. An Alteration Type 1 filing, the kind that changes use or occupancy, starts higher, at a $170 minimum for the first $5,000.

Trade permits are separate filings. Electrical work is priced by the item: a $40 filing fee, then charges per switch, panel, cable run, and outlet past the first ten. So a $25,000 kitchen lands at $254 on the city side — the $227 alteration fee plus a small electrical filing — and a bathroom at $233.20. Some work needs no permit at all: reroofing above the deck, and window swaps that keep the same opening, size, and light and air, are on the no-permit list.

Now the honest limit. The $227 covers one small-house alteration. Alteration filings across the city commonly run from about $500 to $10,000 or more, and new buildings run far higher. Most filings also need a registered architect or engineer to file in DOB NOW — a fee that can pass the city fee on a small job. Ask for two lines: the DOB fee, and the filing professional’s fee. Payment timing changed too: electrical permits, and work with a new or changed certificate of occupancy, now pay 50% at filing and the rest later.

How much does a building permit cost in San Francisco?

San Francisco is the expensive city in this trio. A building permit there is two charges on the same value: a plan review fee, plus an issuance fee. Both come from Table 1A-A, in the July 2026 edition under Ordinance 112-26, in force for permits issued on or after July 12, 2026.

At $8,000 the two charges add to about $797, and two small state fees ride on top — $4 per $100,000 of value, minimum $1, plus 0.00013 times the value on homes — bringing the permit to $799.50. At $12,000 the total is $947.66. At $25,000 it is $1,429.18. A pre-1979 one- or two-family home adds a flat $5 lead-safe-work charge.

$799.50 on an $8,000 project; $1,429.18 on a $25,000 project — plan review plus issuance, state fees included

Then the trades bill separately, at flat rates. Most standard electrical jobs file at $305. Plumbing and mechanical permits start at $290 each. A reroof on a single-family home or duplex is a flat $320, up from $309. A demolition permit runs about $725.

Add a real room to see the true total. A $15,000 bathroom, building plus plumbing and electrical permits, comes to $1,653.78. A $25,000 kitchen comes to $2,169.18 — about eight times New York’s city fees on a like-for-like remodel. Work done without a permit can be charged at double the fee, or more, to legalize it. What the city fee does not cover

Whichever city you are in, the permit fee is one line of a longer bill.

Drawings and design. All three cities start from drawings for plan-reviewed work. An architect or designer charges for those, and the fee is not small on a small job.

Trade permits. A remodel that touches electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work can carry three extra fees on top of the building permit.

The filing professional. New York filings need an architect or engineer. In Chicago and San Francisco, many owners hire an expediter for bigger jobs. Our permit expediter cost guide shows what that help runs.

Corrections and re-inspections. A sent-back filing costs time everywhere, and in Chicago an incomplete filing can cost the $600 penalty on top. A failed inspection can add a re-inspection fee — our failed inspection guide lists the New York and Chicago charges.

Time. A cheap fee in a slow queue can cost more than a high fee in a fast one. Our permit timeline guide compares review times and the legal ways to move faster.

How to get your exact number

In Chicago, run the city’s own Permit Fee Calculator, and confirm whether your job files plan-based or through the Express Permit Program. That one choice swings a small bathroom between $602 and about $325.

In New York City, price the alteration off Table 28-112.2 with your true job cost, add the $45 records fee, then list each trade filing by item. Get your architect or engineer’s filing fee in writing, as its own line.

In San Francisco, work from DBI Table 1A-A, and add each trade permit and the state fees. If the value of the work changes, re-run the number after the change.

In all three cities, get the permit lines in the contract, in writing: who files, who pays each fee, and who pays if a filing comes back incomplete or an inspection fails. Our guide on who pulls the permit explains why the name on the permit matters.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Chicago?

A plan-based permit starts at the $602 minimum under the 2026 tables; jobs at $8,000 and $25,000 both pay $602. You pay a $600 non-refundable deposit at filing. Simpler Express Permit work pays lower flat fees of its own.

How much does a building permit cost in New York City for a house?

For a one- to three-family home: $130 for the first $5,000 of job cost, plus $2.60 per added $1,000, plus a $45 records fee — $182.80 at $8,000, $227 at $25,000. Trade permits and your architect or engineer cost extra.

Why is San Francisco’s permit so much more expensive?

It charges twice on value — plan review plus issuance — then bills trade permits separately. A $25,000 project pays $1,429.18 for the building permit alone; a $25,000 kitchen pays $2,169.18 with its trade permits.

Are trade permits included in these prices?

No. The headline figures are the building permit only. Trade permits are separate filings in all three cities — $305 electrical and $290 plumbing or mechanical in San Francisco, $40 plus per-item charges in New York, separate flat-fee tables in Chicago.

Is a Cook County permit the same as a Chicago permit?

No. Chicago issues permits inside the city. Unincorporated Cook County issues its own, with its own e-permit list and fees. Check which office covers your address first.

Does the permit fee include an architect, engineer, or expediter?

No. The city fee pays the city. Drawings, filing professionals, and expediters are separate costs — on a small job they can pass the city fee. Get each one quoted as its own line.
