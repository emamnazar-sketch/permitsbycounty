How much does a building permit cost in Miami-Dade County?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 20, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

If you are checking building permit cost Miami-Dade fees on a county lot, start with square feet. A new single-family house or duplex costs $0.985 per square foot for the building permit under the county fee schedule effective October 1, 2026, plus a $66.69 application fee, with a $150.82 minimum. Alterations and repairs to a house cost $0.50 per square foot, with the same minimum and an $870 maximum. Plumbing, electrical and mechanical permits are priced per square foot too, and a new house also pays impact fees that often run larger than the permit fees themselves.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • New single-family house or duplex in unincorporated Miami-Dade: $0.985 per sq ft for the building permit, plus a $66.69 application fee, $150.82 minimum.
  • Additions to a house pay the same $0.985 per sq ft. Alterations and repairs pay $0.50 per sq ft, capped at $870 for the building fee.
  • Trade permits add $0.369 per sq ft in total: plumbing $0.143, electrical $0.113, mechanical $0.113.
  • All four permits together run $1.354 per sq ft, so a 4,000 sq ft house pays about $5,416 in permit fees before surcharges.
  • A new house also pays impact fees, about $19,700 to $22,900 on a 6,000 sq ft house, and those fees are due in full before the permit is issued.

First, check who issues your permit

Miami-Dade County issues permits for lots in the unincorporated county. If your lot sits inside a city, that city issues the permit and sets its own fees. The City of Miami, for example, prices a residential permit at 0.50 percent of the estimated construction cost, with a $110 minimum and a $40 application fee, under its schedule adopted April 23, 2026. That is a different math path from the county schedule in this post.

So your first step is not a calculator. It is one question: county lot or city lot? The numbers below are the county numbers, from Miami-Dade Implementing Order IO-04-63, effective October 1, 2026. They apply to a house the county permits directly. If a city permits your job, use the county figures to understand the shape of the bill, then check your city’s own schedule for the exact lines.

How building permit cost Miami-Dade math works for a new house

The county prices a new house by floor area. You take the square feet, multiply by $0.985, and add the $66.69 application fee. If that sum lands under $150.82, you pay $150.82. There is no percentage of your build cost in this math, and the size of your construction budget does not change the rate.

Here is a worked example you can check line by line. Take a new single-family house of 2,400 square feet with a $350,000 value. The building permit fee is 2,400 times $0.985, which is $2,364. Add the $66.69 application fee. Then add the county and state surcharges that sit on the permit fee: a $177.30 county RER surcharge, plus two state surcharges of $23.64 and $35.46. The building permit total comes to $2,667.09. Trade permits, impact fees and utility charges sit outside that total, and the next sections price them.

You pay part of the building fee up front. For a new single-family house the upfront fee is $0.616 per square foot, paid when the county gives you a process number. That upfront money is credited toward the final building permit fee, so it is not an extra charge. The county says the upfront fees cover two reviews: one first review and one rework review. If your plans need more rounds than that, ask the county what a further review costs before you resubmit.

Additions and alterations pay different rates

An addition to a single-family house or duplex pays the new-house rate: $0.985 per square foot of the added area, plus the $66.69 application fee, with the $150.82 minimum. A 500 square foot addition therefore costs $492.50 for the building permit before the application fee and surcharges.

Alterations and repairs inside an existing house pay half that rate: $0.50 per square foot, plus the $66.69 application fee. The building fee for an alteration has a floor and a ceiling. You pay at least $150.82 and at most $870. The county prices an alteration on the total square footage of the structure, not just the rooms you touch, so a whole-house figure goes into the math even for a one-room job. Take a 1,000 square foot house: the alteration building fee is $500, plus the application fee, before surcharges and any trade permits.

Other buildings pay a lower per-foot rate on a larger base. New construction and additions for other occupancy groups cost $0.41 per square foot for the first 100,000 square feet. Above that size, the fee is $41,000 for the first 100,000 square feet plus $0.15 for each extra square foot.

Trade permits are priced per square foot too

A new house needs more than the building permit. It needs a plumbing permit, an electrical permit and a mechanical permit for the heating and cooling work. On the county schedule each trade has its own per-foot rate: plumbing $0.143 per square foot, electrical $0.113 per square foot, and mechanical $0.113 per square foot.

Add the three trade rates to the building rate and you get $1.354 per square foot for all four permits. That one number lets you size the permit bill for a new house fast. A 4,000 square foot house pays about $5,416 across the four permits. A 6,000 square foot house pays about $8,124. A 10,000 square foot house pays about $13,540. Surcharges sit on top of those figures, and impact fees sit outside them.

Small jobs do not follow the new-house math. A contractor guide for Miami-Dade puts typical permit costs for common small projects in these ranges for 2026: a roof replacement at $300 to $650, a window or door replacement at $80 to $200 per opening, a heating and cooling system replacement at $180 to $320, and a pool installation at $1,200 to $2,400. Use those ranges to sanity-check a quote, then confirm your exact fee with the county or your city.

Impact fees are the bigger bill on a new house

Here is the part that catches new builders off guard. On a new house, the impact fees are often larger than the permit fees. Impact fees are one-time charges that pay for roads and transit, schools, parks, police and fire service. The county collects them in full before it issues the building permit, so you need that money ready early, not at the end of the job.

For a detached single-family house, the county schedule breaks down like this. The mobility fee, which covers roads, bike routes and transit, runs $9,275 to $10,625 depending on your context zone. The school fee is $612 per home plus $0.918 per gross square foot. The parks fee runs $3,084.21 to $4,902.89 depending on your park district. Police adds a flat $688.42 and fire adds a flat $527.55.

Run those lines for a 6,000 square foot house and the impact fees land between about $19,700 and about $22,900. The permit fees for that same house are about $8,124. Put the two together and you are at roughly $27,800 to $31,000 before surcharges and utility charges. A separate impact fee tracker puts the total for one new single-family home in unincorporated Miami-Dade County at $20,659.76 per unit, which sits inside the same range.

JMK Contractor, a Miami general contractor, gives planning ranges that match this picture. It puts a new single-family home at about $20,000 to $60,000 or more all-in, with impact fees as the largest part. It puts a home addition at about $2,500 to $15,000 or more, because impact fees apply to the new area, and a large or whole-home remodel at about $3,000 to $15,000 or more. A like-for-like remodel that adds no area does not pay impact fees.

What else sits outside the permit fee

Water and sewer connection is a separate county charge. The county water and sewer department charges $1.39 per average daily gallon for water and $5.60 per average daily gallon for sewer, effective October 1, 2026. Your home’s expected daily use sets that bill, so ask for the figure early if you are building new.

Design work is separate too. Plans, surveys and engineer or architect fees are not county permit fees, and Miami-Dade requires a signed and sealed plan package for a new home. Budget those costs next to the county charges, not inside them.

Timing is a cost of its own. JMK Contractor puts a new single-family permit at about 3 to 6 months, an addition at 1 to 4 months, and a large remodel at 1 to 3 months. Those are planning ranges, not promises, but they tell you how long your money sits in the process before work starts.

One last check on dates. The county rates in this post come from the schedule effective October 1, 2026. The county changed parts of its fee orders in 2026 to follow new state law, including a rule that fees may not be figured as a percentage of construction cost. That is part of why the county schedule runs on square feet. Fees change, so confirm your lines on the county’s current schedule the week you file.

How to keep your Miami-Dade permit bill down

Start with the right schedule. County lot or city lot is the first fork, and pricing a city job off the county table, or the other way round, gives you a wrong budget from day one. If you are not sure who permits your lot, ask the county’s building department before you pay anyone to draw plans.

Measure your area with care. The county fee turns on square feet, and the alteration fee turns on the total area of the structure. A sloppy measure moves every per-foot line at once. Measure once, write the figure down, and use the same figure on every form.

Use the upfront review well. Your upfront fee covers a first review and one rework review for each discipline. Send a complete package the first time: signed and sealed plans, the product approvals your windows, doors and roof need, and the forms the county lists for your permit type. A thin package burns your included rework on basic gaps.

Do not add area you do not need. On a new house or an addition, impact fees grow with the new area. If a design choice adds square feet, it adds permit fees and impact fees at the same time. Price that choice with both bills in view.

Ask about impact fees before you close on a lot. The mobility zone and the park district change the total by thousands of dollars from one lot to the next. Two lots at the same price can carry very different county bills. That check costs you one phone call and can save you a surprise in the tens of thousands.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Miami-Dade County for a new house?

On a county lot, the building permit costs $0.985 per square foot plus a $66.69 application fee, with a $150.82 minimum, under the schedule effective October 1, 2026. A 2,400 square foot house pays $2,667.09 for the building permit with its surcharges. Trade permits add $0.369 per square foot, and impact fees on a new house often add $19,700 to $22,900 or more.

How much is a permit for a home addition in Miami-Dade County?

An addition to a single-family house or duplex pays the same building rate as new construction: $0.985 per square foot of added area, plus the $66.69 application fee. A 500 square foot addition costs $492.50 for the building permit before surcharges and trade permits. Impact fees also apply to the new area.

How much does a permit cost for a remodel in Miami-Dade County?

Alterations and repairs to a house cost $0.50 per square foot for the building permit, priced on the total square footage of the structure, with a $150.82 minimum and an $870 maximum, plus the $66.69 application fee. A large or whole-home remodel commonly runs about $3,000 to $15,000 or more once trade permits are counted, per JMK Contractor’s 2026 planning ranges.

Are impact fees included in the Miami-Dade building permit fee?

No. Impact fees are separate charges for roads and transit, schools, parks, police and fire service. On a new detached house they total about $19,700 to $22,900 for a 6,000 square foot home, and the county collects them in full before it issues the building permit.

Does the City of Miami charge the same permit fees as Miami-Dade County?

No. The city sets its own schedule. The City of Miami prices a residential permit at 0.50 percent of the estimated construction cost, with a $110 minimum and a $40 application fee. The county prices by square foot. Which schedule applies depends on whether your lot is in the city or in the unincorporated county.

What does the upfront fee cover in Miami-Dade County?

For a new single-family house the upfront fee is $0.616 per square foot. It is credited toward your final building permit fee, so it is not an extra charge. The county says it covers two reviews: one first review and one rework review per discipline.
