How much does a solar permit cost — and do I need a separate permit for a panel upgrade?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published October 30, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

For most homes, the city or county solar permit fee is a few hundred dollars. Published 2026 examples include $365 in Maplewood, Minnesota — $200 building plus $165 electrical — and $390 in Phoenix, Arizona, and California law caps the total at $450 for a system up to 15 kW, checked October 30, 2026. A main panel upgrade is usually not covered by that fee. It normally needs its own electrical permit, and the work itself often costs thousands. Your installer normally pulls the permits.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • There is no one national price. Each city sets its own fee, and how it counts the fee changes the total.
  • Real 2026 home fees sit in the few-hundred-dollar range below. California caps the total at $450 up to 15 kW. Colorado caps it at $500.
  • A panel upgrade is a separate job in most places, with its own electrical permit and fee, on top of the panel and labor.
  • The fee is not the whole cost. Drawings, engineering, office time, and a re-inspection all add money.
  • Ask before you sign: what does this city charge, in writing, and is a panel upgrade in my quote?

Yes, you need a permit for home solar

A rooftop solar job needs a permit in every U.S. area we checked. The panels bolt to the roof, so the city checks the structure. The system wires into your panel and out to the grid, so it checks the wiring too.

In some places that is one combined solar permit. In others it is two permits — building and electrical — with two fees. Maplewood below is an example. There is also a separate step with your power company, called interconnection: its OK to connect and switch on. It is not part of the city permit.

Your installer normally pulls the permits. In California a licensed solar or electrical contractor pulls it and stays responsible through the final inspection, per the NRG Clean Power 2026 guide, checked October 30, 2026. The same pattern holds in most states. Our guide on who pulls a permit explains why that split matters.

How cities set the solar permit cost and fee

GreenLancer’s 2026 guide, checked October 30, 2026, sorts local fees into three kinds.

A flat fee is one set price, often up to a size limit. It often covers plan check and a set number of inspections.

A tiered fee rises with system size, by size band or by a set amount per kilowatt over a base size. California’s cap works this way over 15 kW.

A value-based fee is a share of the job’s cost, so a pricier system pays more. California bars this for solar: the fee cannot rest on the value of the system or the home.

The city invoice is only part of the cost, though. Drawings, engineering, and office time can cost more than the fee itself.

Galway, New York shows the value-based kind: 0.5% of job cost, $100 minimum, for a home system up to 25 kW — $150 on a $30,000 system, per the same guide.

Colorado caps the total too, at $500 for a home system, through December 31, 2029, per the same guide. Most other states set no cap, and two towns next door can charge very different fees for the same roof.

Do I need a separate permit for a panel upgrade?

Usually, yes. If the job includes a new main panel — often 100 amps to 200 amps — most cities treat it as its own electrical job, with its own permit and fee. GreenLancer’s guide puts it plainly: those permits sit outside the solar fee. Its Lemoore example gives the size of that fee.

$240 for a service panel permit of 400 amps or less, listed apart from the solar fee; the same schedule lists $160 for an EV charger permit and $160 for a re-inspection

The panel permit fee in Lemoore — Lemoore, California · Schedule effective September 1, 2026, checked October 30, 2026

Source: GreenLancer — How Much Does a Solar Permit Cost? 2026 Fees & State Caps, citing the City of Lemoore master fee schedule · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

The NRG Clean Power guide says the same: a main panel upgrade sets off a separate electrical permit.

Why do so many solar jobs need a new panel? Solar feeds power in through a breaker in that panel. An older 100-amp panel may have no safe room for that breaker under the code’s sizing rule. In some cases a different hookup, such as a supply-side tap, or a smaller breaker avoids the upgrade. A newer 200-amp panel with open space often takes solar with no panel work at all.

What the panel upgrade itself costs

Keep two numbers apart. The panel permit fee is small — $240 in Lemoore. The work is not. An electrician swaps the panel, re-lands every circuit, and often coordinates a short shutoff with the utility.

A main panel upgrade as part of a solar job runs $2,500 to $4,500 of work, described as common in homes built before 1990

Panel upgrade work in older homes — California · Guide updated August 5, 2026, checked October 30, 2026

Source: NRG Clean Power — How Do You Get a Solar Permit in California? · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

That range is for the work, not a quote for your house. If your solar quote has a line like “permitting and interconnection: $1,500,” ask what the city actually charges — the gap is the firm’s own costs, not city fees, per the NRG guide.

Check the quote for quiet add-ons too. A battery brings its own permit or fire review in some cities; Lemoore folds storage into the solar fee. Ground mounts can add a zoning check. A failed inspection adds a re-inspection fee.

The fee is not the whole permitting cost

Installers split permitting cost into three piles. Only the first is the city fee: application, plan review, and inspections. The second is the papers — the plan set, any engineering, and a stamp if your city asks for one. The third is office time: filing, answering plan-check comments, and re-filing.

GreenLancer works one sample budget to show the shape of it.

$450 city fee, plus $200 for a plan set, plus $150 for an engineering review if needed, plus $120 of office labor — a $920 total permitting cost

How long does a solar permit take?

In a growing number of cities, a standard home system is approved the day it is filed. SolarAPP+ is a free online tool that checks a design against the code and issues the permit at once if it passes.

California pushed this into law. Under SB 379, most larger cities and counties had to offer automated online permitting for home solar up to 38.4 kW by September 30, 2023, and cities over 5,000 people by September 30, 2024, per the NRG guide. There, permits now come out at once or the same day for standard systems. On the older paper path, the same permit can take days to a few weeks.

The permit is not the finish line. After install, the city inspects. Then the power company gives its OK to switch on. For the big California utilities, the NRG guide puts that wait at 2 to 6 weeks after inspection. Our guide on how long a building permit takes covers the same pattern.

How to keep the permit cost in check, step by step

1. Get your city’s fee in writing before you sign. Ask which fee table the installer used, and check it against the city’s schedule and, in California or Colorado, the state cap.

2. Ask if your panel can take solar as it sits. If an upgrade is needed, ask if a smaller breaker or a supply-side tap avoids it, and what each path costs.

3. Make the quote split its lines. City fees on one line, panel work on its own, battery or charger permits on theirs. A lump line hides the one number you can check.

4. Ask what happens if the first inspection fails. Who pays the re-inspection fee? Lemoore charges $160. Your city will have its own figure.

5. Ask which filing path the firm will use. If your city offers the automated fast path and your roof is standard, ask why the job would not use it. In Lemoore that choice is the gap between $450 and $200.

Common mistakes that raise the bill

Signing a quote that buries the panel upgrade. The price looks low until that line appears mid-job.

Assuming the state cap covers the whole step. It covers city and county charges — not drawings, engineering, office time, California fire district fees, or the panel work.

Forgetting the power company step. A fast city permit does not shorten the interconnection wait.

Changing the system after filing. Different gear than the approved plans show can send the job back through plan check and add a re-inspection fee.

Letting an unpermitted system ride. It can draw fines, an order to remove the work, and trouble at sale. Our guide on building without a permit sets out the fix, and it costs more than the permit would have.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a solar permit cost?

Often a few hundred dollars in city fees. Published 2026 examples include $365 in Maplewood and $390 in Phoenix. California caps the total at $450 up to 15 kW, and Colorado at $500.

Do I need a separate permit for a panel upgrade?

Usually, yes. A new main panel is its own electrical job in most cities, with its own permit and fee — $240 in Lemoore for a panel of 400 amps or less.

Who pulls the solar permit — me or the installer?

The installer, in almost every case. A licensed contractor files it and stays responsible through the final inspection.

Does every solar job need a panel upgrade?

No. A newer 200-amp panel with open space often takes solar as it sits. Older 100-amp panels are the common case.

Is the power company’s approval part of the permit?

No. Interconnection is a separate approval, given after the city inspection. In California that wait is often 2 to 6 weeks with the big utilities.

What if my quote shows a permit fee over the California cap?

Ask for the itemized invoice. The $450 cap covers the sum of city and county charges up to 15 kW. A city can charge more only with an adopted written finding proving its real costs.
