How can subcontractors get on a GC’s bid list before a project goes to bid?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published October 27, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

Subcontractors get on a general contractor’s bid list before a project goes to bid by doing three things early: complete a full profile on the platforms and portals GCs search, prequalify with each target GC before a job is live, and reach out to the GC’s estimating team while the project is still in planning. ConstructConnect, checked October 27, 2026, says project databases surface opportunities 6 to 24 months before a formal bid is issued, and that specialty contractors use that early view to reach out to GCs before bid lists are set. By the time an invitation to bid posts, the list is usually already built.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • A bid list is built before the bid. Waiting for the invitation means you are asking after the roster is set.
  • Early projects show up 6 to 24 months before a formal bid, per ConstructConnect. Design development, about 6 to 12 months out, is often the practical window for a sub to make first contact.
  • GCs only invite subs they can vet fast. License, insurance, bonding, safety record and past work are the core of prequalification, per Procore.
  • The working routine is three steps, per ConstructConnect: complete your profiles, prequalify early, and follow up after every bid, win or lose.
  • Permits help you pick which GCs to target. A GC pulling permits again and again in your trade and your area is active now, not someday.

Why is the bid list already set when the invitation posts?

A bid board and a project database are not the same thing, and the difference is the whole point of this post. ConstructConnect explains it in plain terms.

A bid board lists projects that are actively out to bid, where the invitation has already gone out and the competition is open; a project database tracks projects from conceptual through post-bid, so contractors can pursue them months before the bid board sees them

If your first sight of a job is the bid board, you are late by design. The GC has already picked who gets the invitation. A sub who is not on that list can sometimes still get in, but they are asking for an exception. A sub who was known, profiled and prequalified months earlier does not need an exception. They are on the list the invitation goes to.

How early can you see a project before it goes to bid?

Earlier than most subs think. The same ConstructConnect guide gives a stage-by-stage clock. Use it to time your outreach, not just to admire how early the data starts.

Opportunities can surface 6 to 24 months before a formal bid is issued; conceptual projects can appear 12 to 24 or more months out, schematic design 9 to 18 months out, design development 6 to 12 months out, and permit filing 3 to 9 months before the bid

Source: ConstructConnect — How Contractors Find Early-Stage Projects · Date pulled: October 27, 2026

That clock also tells you when to move. For a trade contractor, ConstructConnect calls design development the practical sweet spot in many cases: the project is real, the design team is mostly set, and sub relationships are still forming. Permit filing is your other clear signal, at about 3 to 9 months before the bid. Do not treat very early as always better — a project found two years out can stall or die. Put your best hours on the nearer window.

What are the three steps to get on the list?

ConstructConnect’s 2026 playbook for connecting with GCs gives the short version, and it matches how GCs describe their own process.

Getting on a general contractor’s bid list can be summarized in three steps: complete your profiles on professional networks and platforms, prequalify with GC firms early, and follow up with them consistently, whether your bid wins or not

The three steps onto a bid list — United States · Playbook checked October 27, 2026; published April 9, 2026, updated April 13, 2026

Source: ConstructConnect — How to Connect with GCs Who Need Subcontractors: A 2026 Playbook · Date pulled: October 27, 2026

Step 1: Complete your profiles everywhere a GC might look. GCs build sub lists from platform searches, existing relationships and referrals, per that same playbook. An empty profile is not neutral — it is a reason to skip you. Fill in your trades, service area, project types, license details and a named estimating contact, with the same facts on every platform.

Step 2: Prequalify before there is a job. Check each target GC’s website for a subcontractor or vendor registration page, and be ready with the documents in the next section. Some large GCs run their own portal, and you need to be in it before they will invite you.

Step 3: Reach out to estimating, and follow up after every bid. Contact the estimating team before bid day, attend pre-bid meetings when you can, and submit clean bids with clear scope. Then follow up whether you won or lost. GCs keep subs who are responsive and easy to work with, and a short follow-up after a loss proves it.

What does prequalification actually ask for?

Prequalification is the GC’s check that you can do the work, carry the risk and finish the job. Procore, which runs prequalification tools for GCs, lists what a high-quality application collects.

A prequalification application collects basic company details, trade licensing, insurance, surety and bonding capacity, safety record including OSHA rates and EMR, finances, project history, litigation history, and capacity including workforce and backlog

What prequalification covers — United States · Guide checked October 27, 2026; last updated June 11, 2024

Source: Procore — Subcontractor Prequalification: The Keys to Selecting Quality Subs · Date pulled: October 27, 2026

Put those papers in one folder before you need them: license numbers and expiry dates, a current certificate of insurance, bonding capacity and single-project limit, EMR and OSHA summary, recent financial statements, and similar finished jobs with values and contacts. GCs may also ask for a W-9 and a work-in-progress schedule. The larger the contract, the more they ask for, per Procore. Safety is not a side box — it is often the first screen.

In a study of 52 GC firms cited by ConstructConnect, 93 percent included safety outcomes in prequalification, 75 percent included questions about safety management, and 60 percent included questions about worker training

Safety in prequalification — United States · Study from 2018, cited in playbook checked October 27, 2026

Source: ConstructConnect — How to Connect with GCs Who Need Subcontractors: A 2026 Playbook, citing a 2018 study of 52 GC firms · Date pulled: October 27, 2026

That study is from 2018, so treat it as a picture of how GCs screen, not a 2026 count. The lesson still holds: a clean safety record you can hand over fast is part of getting invited. Procore also notes that established GCs typically only allow prequalified subcontractors to bid. No bid-day effort fixes a missing prequalification.

How do permits help you pick which GCs to target?

You cannot prequalify with every GC, and you should not try. Register with enough GCs to keep your pipeline active without burying your estimating team — ConstructConnect’s own rule of thumb. So pick targets with evidence.

Permit records are one honest source. When a GC pulls permits in your county, for work like yours, again and again, that GC is active in your market right now. Permits also show the address, work type and timing, so your first call can name real jobs that GC is already running.

Use permits alongside early project data. A database can show a job months before a permit exists; a permit confirms someone paid a fee and filed plans. Ten GCs you have fully profiled, prequalified with and called is worth more than a hundred you emailed once.

What should your capability statement say?

When a GC asks who you are in one page, send a capability statement, not a brochure. ConstructConnect lists what it should hold: core trades, service area, license and insurance details, bonding capacity, safety metrics, key project experience, notable clients, and the best estimating contact. Keep it to one page of plain, checkable facts, and update it the same week a license, bonding limit or flagship job changes.

What are the common mistakes that keep subs off the list?

First, waiting for the invitation. If you only wait, you are limited to GCs who already know you, as ConstructConnect puts it. The best subs also find projects themselves.

Second, a stale prequalification. If a GC checks you the week a list is built and your insurance certificate is out of date, you look risky at the exact moment it costs you. Put renewals on a calendar and refresh before they lapse.

Third, bidding everything. Bid work that fits your trade, size and area, and say no fast to work that does not. Your yes then means more.

Fourth, a messy bid and no follow-up. A clean bid with clear scope, on time, plus a short follow-up after the result, is how a first invitation becomes a standing place on the list.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get on a general contractor’s bid list?

Do the three steps ConstructConnect names: complete your profiles on the platforms and portals GCs search, prequalify with each target GC early, and follow up consistently after every bid, win or lose. Start before a project is live, not on bid day.

How far before a bid should I reach out to a GC?

Use the project clock. Conceptual projects can be 12 to 24 months out, and design development is about 6 to 12 months before the bid. For most subs, that design development window, or the permit filing window at about 3 to 9 months out, is the practical time to make first contact.

What documents do I need to prequalify with a GC?

Have your trade license, certificate of insurance, bonding capacity, safety record and EMR, financial statements, and a list of similar finished projects ready in one folder. Larger jobs ask for more, including a W-9 and work-in-progress schedule, per Procore.

How many GCs should I register with?

Enough to keep your pipeline active without overwhelming your estimating team — ConstructConnect’s own rule of thumb. For most small trade firms, that means a short, well-worked target list, not every GC in the state.

Can I get on a bid list without paying for a platform?

Yes, to start. Free profiles on networks like PlanHub and BuildingConnected, direct prequalification on GC websites, and free permit reports can get you in front of local GCs. Paid databases mainly buy earlier sight of more, and larger, projects.

What should I do after I lose a bid?

Follow up. Ask, briefly, what decided it, thank the estimator, and confirm you want the next job that fits. GCs keep responsive, low-drama subs on preferred lists, and a clean follow-up after a loss is one of the clearest signals of that.
