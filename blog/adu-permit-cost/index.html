How much does an ADU permit cost — and how long does it take?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published October 26, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

In California, city fees for an ADU permit usually run $2,000 to $15,000, and the review usually takes 4 to 12 weeks after you file a complete application. ADUscale, checked October 26, 2026, gives that $2,000 to $15,000 range and that 4-to-12-week timeline. California law says a city must approve or deny a complete ADU application within 60 days. The full cost to get permit-ready is higher, because plans, engineering, and utility work sit on top of the city fees.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • City permit fees alone often run $2,000 to $15,000 in California. Your city sets the exact number.
  • Review often takes 4 to 12 weeks after a complete filing. California law sets a 60-day limit for that decision.
  • An ADU under 750 square feet pays no impact fees in California. That one size choice can save real money.
  • San Francisco is the high end. One city guide puts its total permit costs at $33,000 to $57,000, with a 90-to-150-day timeline.
  • Austin is lower and slower in a different way. One Austin guide puts total permit costs at $6,000 to $16,000, and review commonly runs 2 to 4 months.

What is an ADU permit?

An ADU permit is the building permit that lets you build an accessory dwelling unit on your lot. That is a second, smaller home: a detached backyard unit, an attached unit, a garage conversion, or a junior ADU inside the main house.

Every ADU needs one. ADUscale is plain about this: every ADU built in California needs a building permit, whether it is detached, attached, a junior ADU, or a garage conversion, and there are no exceptions. Building first and asking later is the expensive path. Our guide on what happens if you build without a permit shows why.

The ADU permit cost in this post means the permit part only, not the build. Plans and construction cost far more than the permit. Keeping those two numbers apart is the first step to a budget that holds.

How much does an ADU permit cost?

There is no single price. Each city sets its own fees, and the same unit can cost very different amounts two towns apart. Start with the California range, then check your own city before you trust any number.

$2,000 to $15,000 in total permit fees, depending on the city

California ADU permit fees — California · Guide checked October 26, 2026

Source: ADUscale — ADU Permit in California: Cost, Process & Timeline (2026 Guide) · Date pulled: October 26, 2026

Inside that range, the pieces are mostly the same everywhere. ADUscale breaks the city fees down like this: plan check, $500 to $3,000; the building permit itself, $1,000 to $5,000; school impact fees, $0 to $5,000; utility connection, $0 to $3,000; and development impact fees, $0 to $4,000. Add your city’s version of each line. That sum is your permit fee.

City examples from the same guide show the spread. Los Angeles averages $8,000 to $12,000. San Diego runs $5,000 to $10,000. Sacramento runs $3,000 to $6,000. A smaller, cheaper city and a big coastal city are not playing the same game, even for the same small home.

Why is San Francisco so much higher?

San Francisco is the outlier most people hear about, and the numbers back that up. A city-by-city drafting guide puts San Francisco at the top of California, and it counts more than the city’s own fees.

$33,000 to $57,000 in total permit costs; approval timeline 90 to 150 days

San Francisco total ADU permit costs — San Francisco, CA · City guide checked October 26, 2026

Source: Andalusia Drafting — City-Specific ADU Permit Requirements · Date pulled: October 26, 2026

Read that figure with care. That guide’s wider breakdown says total California ADU permit costs run $12,000 to $77,000 once you count direct permit fees, professional soft costs like plans and engineering, and utility connection costs. San Francisco’s $33,000 to $57,000 sits inside that wider way of counting. It is not just the check you write to the city.

The lesson is not that San Francisco is a bad place to build an ADU. The lesson is to ask which costs a quote covers. A $5,000 quote that covers only city fees and a $40,000 quote that covers fees, plans, engineering, and utilities can describe the same project.

What does an ADU permit cost in Austin?

Austin is one of our live metros, and it shows how a non-California city prices the same job. There is no state 60-day rule and no 750-square-foot rule in Texas. The city fees, the impact fees, and the review clock all work differently.

$6,000 to $16,000 in total permit costs: building permit application $2,000 to $5,000, plan review $500 to $1,500, water and wastewater impact fees $3,000 to $8,000, and trade permits for electrical, plumbing, and HVAC at $200 to $500 each

Austin total ADU permit costs — Austin, TX · Guide checked October 26, 2026

Source: Neuhaus Realty Group — ADU Guide Austin TX (2026) · Date pulled: October 26, 2026

Notice the big line in Austin. Impact fees for water and wastewater can be the largest single item, bigger than the building permit itself. The same guide warns that those fees change with your lot and with utility capacity, so a neighbor’s total tells you very little about yours.

A second Austin source gives a lower, narrower count. A Texas ADU guide that tracks city rules puts total Austin permit-related costs, counting plan review, the building permit, and impact fees, at roughly $3,000 to $15,000. It also cites an Austin benchmark for the city’s own Development Services fees on a sample single-unit project. Treat both guides as planning ranges, then get the city’s own fee estimate for your address. That estimate is the only number you should budget against.

How long does an ADU permit take?

In California, plan for 4 to 12 weeks from a complete application to approval. That is ADUscale’s timeline, and the city examples inside it show the spread: Sacramento at 4 to 6 weeks, San Diego at 6 to 10 weeks, and Los Angeles at 8 to 12 weeks.

A local agency must approve or deny a complete ADU application within 60 days; if it does not, the application is deemed approved

California 60-day ADU rule — California · State law checked October 26, 2026

Source: California Government Code §65852.2 via California Legislative Information · Date pulled: October 26, 2026

That 60-day clock has a catch most people miss. It starts when the application is complete, not on the day you first send something in. A file that is missing plans, calculations, or forms does not start the clock. ADUscale calls missing documents the number one cause of permit delays, and its list of what a complete file needs is long: the application form, a site plan, architectural plans, structural engineering stamped by a licensed California engineer, Title 24 energy calculations, a utility connection plan, and proof that you own the property.

San Francisco runs longer than the state pattern. The city guide above gives it 90 to 150 days. Austin runs longer in its own way: the Texas guide that tracks Austin says plan review commonly runs about 2 to 4 months, depending on how complete the file is and how complex the project is.

And the permit clock is not the whole project clock. Design and engineering come before you can even file. ADUscale puts design and engineering at 4 to 8 weeks, before the 4-to-12-week review. So a honest plan from first drawing to permit in hand is often 3 to 6 months, even when the city’s part goes well.

How can you keep the ADU permit cost down?

Start with size. In California, size is money in a very direct way.

No impact fee may be charged for an ADU under 750 square feet; at 750 square feet or more, impact fees must be charged in proportion to the size of the main house

California impact-fee exemption under 750 square feet — California · State law checked October 26, 2026

Source: California Government Code §65852.2 via California Legislative Information · Date pulled: October 26, 2026

ADUscale makes the same point in plain terms: ADUs under 750 square feet are exempt from school and development impact fees under California law. If your plan sits just over that line, ask your designer what staying under it does to the fee total. Sometimes a slightly smaller unit is a much cheaper unit to permit.

Next, file a complete application the first time. Every missing page costs you weeks, and weeks cost money when a builder, a loan, or a tenant is waiting. Use the city’s own checklist, not a general one, and check every item off before you submit.

Then ask about pre-approved plans. Some California cities keep a set of ready-made ADU plans that have already passed review. ADUscale notes that state law gives pre-approved plans a shorter path, and the Los Angeles example in the city guides shows a city with more than 20 free standard plans. A plan the city has already approved cannot be sent back for the same fixes a fresh plan can.

Last, get the utility answer early. In Austin, water and wastewater impact fees were the biggest line in the breakdown. In California, ADUscale puts utility connection at $0 to $3,000, and the wider drafting guide puts utility costs as high as $35,000 on hard lots. A short call to the utility before you design can tell you which world your lot lives in.

What happens if you skip the ADU permit?

Do not. An unpermitted ADU cannot be legally rented, may not be covered by insurance, and creates problems at a sale. ADUscale adds the money side: city fines can run up to $25,000 or more, and getting a retroactive permit later typically costs 2 to 3 times more than permitting before you build.

If the work is already done, the fix is a retroactive permit. Our guide on how to get a retroactive permit for work that is already done walks through those steps in order. It is slower and costs more than doing it in the right order, but it is the path that makes the unit legal.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an ADU permit cost?

In California, city fees usually run $2,000 to $15,000, depending on the city. Los Angeles averages $8,000 to $12,000 and Sacramento $3,000 to $6,000, per ADUscale. Plans, engineering, and utility work cost extra, and San Francisco’s total permit costs can reach $33,000 to $57,000.

How long does it take to get an ADU permit?

In California, 4 to 12 weeks from a complete application is the usual range, and state law requires a decision within 60 days. San Francisco can take 90 to 150 days. In Austin, plan review commonly runs about 2 to 4 months. Design time before you file is extra.

Do I need a permit for a garage conversion ADU?

Yes. Every ADU needs a building permit, including a garage conversion and a junior ADU. ADUscale states there are no exceptions in California. The permit is what makes the unit legal to rent and to insure.

Why does an ADU under 750 square feet cost less to permit in California?

State law bars cities from charging impact fees on an ADU under 750 square feet. At 750 square feet or more, impact fees apply in proportion to the main house. Staying under that line removes a whole set of fees.

What is included in the ADU permit cost?

The city part usually covers plan check, the building permit, and any school, impact, and utility fees that apply to your size and lot. It does not cover your designer, your engineer, or construction. Ask any quote to say which of those it covers.

Can a city deny my ADU permit in California?

State law limits denial. If a complete application follows state law and local rules, the city must approve or deny it within 60 days, and a missed deadline means the application is deemed approved. A denial must come with a written list of what is wrong and how to fix it.
