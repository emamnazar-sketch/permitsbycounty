How much does a building permit cost in Philadelphia?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 20, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

If you are checking building permit cost Philadelphia fees, start with the building permit itself. A new one- or two-family house costs a flat $1,328 under the City of Philadelphia fee schedule effective October 1, 2026. A home alteration costs $76 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 for each extra 100 square feet. Additions cost $75 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 per extra 100 square feet. Electrical, plumbing and other trade permits cost extra, and small city and state surcharges apply per permit.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • New one- or two-family house in Philadelphia: $1,328 flat for the building permit.
  • Home alterations: $76 for the first 500 sq ft, plus $56 for each extra 100 sq ft.
  • Additions and accessory structures for a house: $75 for the first 500 sq ft, plus $56 per extra 100 sq ft.
  • Trade permits are separate: electrical is $25 per $1,000 of electrical cost, and a plumbing alteration is $50 for the first 7 fixtures.
  • Budget for the filing fee, $3 city and $4.50 state surcharges per permit, and zoning or other permits your project may need.

The short answer, by project

Philadelphia prices most house work by floor area, not by what the job costs you. That is good news if you want a clear number early. You measure the area the work covers, you look up the line for your project type, and you do simple math.

A new house is the simplest line on the schedule. The city charges one flat fee, $1,328, for new construction of a one- or two-family dwelling. The size of the house does not change that building permit fee. Other buildings pay by area: $253 for the first 500 square feet, plus $73 for each extra 100 square feet.

An addition to a house costs $75 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 for each extra 100 square feet, or part of 100. So a 400 square foot addition costs $75 for the building permit. A 700 square foot addition costs $75 plus two times $56, which is $187. The trade permits for wiring and plumbing inside that addition come on top of that.

Alterations and repairs inside a house cost $76 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 for each extra 100 square feet. A 300 square foot kitchen and bath remodel that stays inside 500 square feet costs $76 for the building permit. A 900 square foot alteration costs $76 plus four times $56, which is $300.

You may ask the city to price an alteration at 2 percent of the cost of construction instead. That choice has a $253 minimum, so it rarely helps a small house job. It can help on a large-area job with a low build cost, but run both sums before you ask.

How building permit cost Philadelphia math works for other buildings

If your building is not a one- or two-family house, the base fees are higher. New construction and additions for other occupancies cost $253 for the first 500 square feet, plus $73 for each extra 100 square feet. Alterations for other occupancies cost $253 for the first 500 square feet, plus $60 for each extra 100 square feet.

A first tenant fit-out of a newly built commercial space has its own line: $16.40 per 100 square feet, with a $189 minimum. A foundation-only permit costs $253 for an area up to 500 square feet, $442 for 501 to 2,500 square feet, and $759 above 2,500 square feet. Those are building permit fees only. Your project may also need zoning approval, trade permits, and other city reviews, each with its own charge.

MatrixGC, in its Philadelphia permit fees guide for 2026, makes the same point in plain terms: for a typical home renovation, the building permit is $76 for the first 500 square feet plus $56 for each extra 100, or 2 percent of construction cost if you ask for that method, with a $253 minimum, and a new house is $1,328. Its guide also notes that the city revised its fee summary in July 2026, but the residential fees did not change from the schedule that took effect on January 1, 2025.

Trade permits are separate, and they add up

The building permit does not cover the trades. If your job includes electrical work, plumbing work, or mechanical work, each trade needs its own permit and its own fee. This is the part that surprises people who budgeted only for the headline building fee.

Electrical work costs $25 for each $1,000, or part of $1,000, of the estimated electrical construction cost. The minimum is $63 and the maximum is $18,975. So $4,000 of electrical work costs $100 for the electrical permit. $10,000 of electrical work costs $250. A rough-in permit, when you need one ahead of the full job, costs $150.

Plumbing is priced by fixtures. For new construction, a plumbing permit costs $284 for the first 7 fixtures, plus $25 for each fixture above 7. For an alteration or an addition to a one- or two-family house, it costs $50 for the first 7 fixtures, plus $22.50 for each fixture above 7. A simple fixture replacement with no piping work costs $31 for a one- or two-family house. A water heater and drain work have their own small lines on the schedule, so ask which line your job falls under before you file.

PermitCalculator, which builds its city tables from published fee schedules, lists common Philadelphia project totals that include the trade pieces: a bathroom at $223.50 and a kitchen at $273.50. Those totals are not the city’s building fee alone. They show what happens when you add the building, electrical, and plumbing pieces for a defined small project. Your total will move with your scope, your fixture count, and your electrical cost.

The small charges people forget

On top of the permit fee itself, Philadelphia adds small fixed charges. City service pages for building permits list a city surcharge of $3 per permit and a state surcharge of $4.50 per permit. There is also a record retention fee of $4 for each page larger than 8.5 by 14 inches in your plan set. None of these will break a budget on their own, but a job with a building permit, an electrical permit, and a plumbing permit pays the surcharges on each permit.

You also pay a filing fee when you apply. For a one- or two-family house, the filing fee is $25. For other buildings, it is $100. The filing fee is not extra profit for the city on top of your permit. It counts toward the final permit fee, and it is not refundable. City pages state that rule in plain words: the fee is paid at application and applied toward the permit fee.

A certificate of occupancy that comes as part of the building permit process carries no added fee on the schedule. If you need a standalone certificate of occupancy, the schedule lists $253 per space. A temporary certificate costs $379. Check which case is yours before you add a line to your budget.

If time matters more than money, the city sells speed. Accelerated plan review gets your application reviewed within 5 business days. The city’s request form says $350 is due at application, with the rest due on approval: a further $1,650 for a building or combination permit review, or a further $700 for other permit types. The accelerated fee does not replace the permit fee, and it is not refundable. For most house jobs, standard review is the right call. For a commercial job where each week of delay costs rent, the math can look different.

Worked examples you can copy

Take a 350 square foot bathroom and kitchen refresh in a rowhouse, with no walls moved and work inside one area. The building permit is $76, because the area is under 500 square feet. If the electrical work is priced at $3,000, the electrical permit is $75, because you pay $25 for each $1,000. If the plumbing touches 5 fixtures, the plumbing permit is $50, because 5 fixtures sit inside the first 7. Before surcharges, the three permits total $201. That is close to the kind of total PermitCalculator shows for its defined bathroom project, and it shows why the building fee alone is not your permit budget.

Take a 600 square foot addition to a house. The building permit is $75 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 for the one extra 100, so $131. If the addition has 8 plumbing fixtures, the plumbing permit is $50 plus $22.50 for the eighth fixture, so $72.50. If the electrical work is $6,000, the electrical permit is $150. Before surcharges and any zoning fee, you are at $353.50.

Take a new single-family house. The building permit is $1,328 flat. New construction plumbing for a house with 12 fixtures is $284 plus five times $25, so $409. Electrical work priced at $20,000 costs $500. Before surcharges, zoning, and any other permits your lot needs, the three permits total $2,237. The house itself will cost far more than that to build, which is the point: in Philadelphia, the permit fees for a house are a small, clear slice of the project, as long as you count every permit the job needs.

What does not sit inside the building permit fee

Zoning is separate. Some projects need a zoning permit before or with the building permit, and zoning has its own fees. A project that needs a zoning appeal, a historic review, or another department’s approval can add fees and time that the building schedule does not show.

Design costs are separate too. Plans, surveys, and engineer or architect work are not city permit fees. Build a small cushion for those items.

Demolition has its own line if your project starts with taking a building down. General demolition costs $25.30 per 100 square feet, with a $253 minimum and a $50,600 maximum on the schedule, and a complete demolition needs a separate permit by a licensed demolition contractor. If your job is a teardown and a rebuild, budget the demolition permit and the new construction permit as two items.

One more warning on timing. The schedule in this post is the one effective October 1, 2026, revised in July 2026. MatrixGC notes the residential lines match the schedule that took effect January 1, 2025. Fees do change, and the city posts a new summary when they do. Check the current summary on the city’s site the week you file, and use the numbers here to spot a quote that looks far off, not to argue with the clerk.

How to keep your Philadelphia permit bill down

File a complete application the first time. Missing plans and missing forms cost you weeks, and weeks cost more than any fee on the schedule. Read the city’s page for your exact permit type before you file, because each page lists the forms, the filing fee, and the surcharges for that permit.

Measure your area with care. The building fee turns on floor area, and a sloppy measure can push you into the next 100 square foot step or start an argument at review. Round the way the schedule does: each extra 100 square feet, or part of it, counts as a full step.

Count your fixtures before you file. Plumbing turns on fixture count, and the step from 7 fixtures to 8 is a real step in price. If a fixture is not in the scope, do not list it. If it is in the scope, list it now. Adding it later means an amended permit, and the schedule lists an amended permit at $25 for an owner-occupied one- or two-family dwelling, with a per-sheet minimum that can reach $94, and $50 if no plans are involved for other cases.

Skip accelerated review unless delay has a price you can name. It is a fair product for a job that loses money each week it waits. It is a poor buy for a bathroom remodel that can wait for standard review.

Ask about the 2 percent option only with both sums in hand. For a small house alteration, the $253 minimum makes it dearer than the area fee. For a big commercial area with a light build cost, it may save you money. The choice is yours to ask for, so do the math first.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Philadelphia for a new house?

A new one- or two-family house costs a flat $1,328 for the building permit under the schedule effective October 1, 2026. Trade permits, surcharges, and any zoning fees are extra. New construction plumbing, for example, starts at $284 for the first 7 fixtures.

How much is a permit for a home remodel in Philadelphia?

A home alteration costs $76 for the first 500 square feet, plus $56 for each extra 100 square feet. A remodel under 500 square feet therefore costs $76 for the building permit, plus trade permits: electrical at $25 per $1,000 of electrical cost and plumbing at $50 for the first 7 fixtures on an alteration.

Are electrical and plumbing permits included in the building permit fee?

No. Each trade has its own permit and fee. Electrical work costs $25 per $1,000 of estimated electrical cost, with a $63 minimum. Plumbing for a house alteration costs $50 for the first 7 fixtures, plus $22.50 for each fixture above 7.

What extra charges sit on top of the permit fee in Philadelphia?

A filing fee of $25 for a one- or two-family house, or $100 for other buildings, paid at application and counted toward the permit fee; a $3 city surcharge and a $4.50 state surcharge per permit; and a $4 record retention fee per large plan page. Zoning and other approvals, where your project needs them, cost extra.

Can I choose to pay 2 percent of my construction cost instead?

For alterations, yes, at your request. The schedule says alteration fees may be based on 2 percent of the cost of construction at the applicant’s request, with a $253 minimum. For most small house jobs the area fee is lower, so compare both before you ask.

How much does faster review cost in Philadelphia?

Accelerated plan review is reviewed within 5 business days. You pay $350 at application, then $1,650 more on approval for a building or combination permit, or $700 more for other permit types. The fee is on top of the permit fee and is not refundable.
