How much does a building permit cost in Wake County and Raleigh?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 26, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

The building permit cost Wake County Raleigh homeowners pay depends on which office issues the permit. In unincorporated Wake County, and in Knightdale, Rolesville, Wendell and Zebulon, the county charges $75 plus $0.25 per square foot of gross floor area for a new one- or two-family home, and that one fee covers all trades. Inside the City of Raleigh, the city charges 0.38% of the project’s calculated value, with a $124 minimum per trade, plus plan review and a 4% technology fee, under the FY27 Development Fee Guide effective July 1, 2026. Same county, two very different bills — so check your address first.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Wake County’s residential fee is simple: $75 plus $0.25 per square foot of gross floor area, all trades included.
  • A 2,000 sq ft county home therefore runs $575 in permit fees before any other charge.
  • Raleigh does it by value, not by size: 0.38% of calculated value, $124 minimum per trade, plus plan review at 57% for new work and a 4% technology fee.
  • A Raleigh new home priced from the city’s own tables totals about $3,917 in building and trade permit fees in the worked example below.
  • Raleigh, Cary, Apex and the other towns run their own counters. A Raleigh mailing address does not always mean the Raleigh fee schedule, and it never means the county one inside city limits.

First question: county or city?

This trips up almost everyone who moves to the Triangle. Raleigh sits inside Wake County, so people assume one permit office covers the whole county. It does not.

Wake County Permits and Inspections issues building permits for the unincorporated parts of the county — the land outside any town or city limits — and, by contract, for four eastern towns: Knightdale, Rolesville, Wendell and Zebulon. If your lot is in one of those places, the county schedule in the next section is your schedule.

If your lot is inside Raleigh, the City of Raleigh’s Planning and Development Department issues your permit, and the city uses its own fee guide. Cary, Apex, Holly Springs, Fuquay-Varina, Garner and Wake Forest each run their own offices too, with their own schedules. This post covers the two biggest systems — the county and Raleigh — because most Wake County addresses fall under one of them.

There is one more wrinkle. Raleigh also has an extraterritorial jurisdiction, land just outside the city limits that still follows Raleigh zoning and building rules. If you are near the city edge, do not guess. Check the address with the county and the city before you budget. Five minutes on that check saves you from pricing the wrong schedule.

How Wake County charges: one fee, all trades

The county system is the simpler of the two, and it is worth saying plainly how simple it is.

You pay a $75 base fee plus $0.25 for every square foot of gross floor area. That is the whole formula for a new house. The fee covers all the trades — building, electrical, plumbing and mechanical — and it even covers the temporary electrical pole you need during construction. There is no separate trade stack to add up.

Gross floor area has a specific meaning here, and it matters. The county measures everything finished inside the inside perimeter of the exterior walls, with no deduction for the garage, corridors, stairs or closets. So a house that feels like 1,800 square feet of living space can measure well over 2,000 square feet on the county’s ruler if the garage sits inside that perimeter. Measure the way the county measures before you run your numbers.

Worked examples make the formula real. A 1,600 sq ft home runs $75 plus $400, so $475. A 2,000 sq ft home runs $75 plus $500, so $575. A 2,800 sq ft home runs $75 plus $700, so $775. Even a large house stays under $1,000 on the county schedule. If someone quotes you several thousand dollars for a county building permit on a plain house, ask them to show you the line items — something else is in that number.

Additions, alterations and accessory buildings use the same formula: $75 plus $0.25 per square foot of gross floor area. A 400 sq ft addition therefore runs $175 in county permit fees. That is a strikingly low number, and it is the county’s real schedule, not a typo.

County flat fees: the small jobs

Not everything in the county runs on square footage. A set of common jobs carries flat fees, and they are easy to budget.

A residential trade permit — a single permit covering the trades on a smaller job — is a flat $75, no matter how many trades are involved, and the electrical pole fee is included in it. A manufactured, modular or moved house is also a flat $75. Residential demolition is a flat $75.

Pools sit a step up. PermitFlow’s Wake County guide, working from the county schedule, lists an in-ground pool permit at $300. That is still a single clear number you can put in a budget on day one.

The pattern to notice: the county keeps its residential fees low and flat where it can. The trade-off is that the county fee is rarely your whole government bill. Zoning, septic, well, driveway and utility charges can sit outside the building permit, and the county’s own planning fees are a separate schedule. The building permit is cheap. The full cost of getting permission to build is the permit plus those neighbours.

How Raleigh charges: a percentage of value

Raleigh runs a completely different system. Forget square-foot rates. The city prices your building permit as a percentage of the project’s calculated value.

Under the FY27 Development Fee Guide, effective July 1, 2026, the building permit fee is 0.38% of calculated value, with a $124 minimum. That minimum applies per trade, so a small job that touches building, electrical and plumbing can hit three $124 minimums before anything else is added.

Calculated value is the city’s number, not yours. Raleigh works it out from its own building valuation tables — for a one- or two-family home, the tables price floor area by construction type. In one worked example from the city’s tables, a 400 sq ft addition is valued at $61,644, which is 400 square feet at $154.11 per square foot. Your contract price and the city’s calculated value are two different figures, and the fee follows the city’s.

Then come the add-ons. Plan review is charged on top of the permit fee — the city’s fee calculator puts it at 57% of the building permit fee for new construction and 55% for alterations. A 4% technology fee is added on top of the fees. A residential zoning permit, where it applies, is $261 before the technology fee, so about $271 with it. And if an inspector comes out and the work is not ready, a failed or not-ready inspection runs $127 per trade.

None of this is hidden. It is all in the city’s fee guide and its fee calculator. But no single line tells you your total, which is why Raleigh totals surprise people who only heard the 0.38% figure.

Building permit cost Wake County Raleigh: two worked Raleigh examples

Here is what the Raleigh math looks like on real projects, using the city’s own rates.

Take a 400 sq ft room addition first. The city values it at $61,644 from its tables. The building permit is 0.38% of that, so $234.25. Plan review at 57% adds $133.52. The technology fee at 4% adds $14.71. Total: $382.48 for the building side of the permit. Trade permits for any electrical, plumbing or mechanical work are charged on top of that.

Now take a new single-family home. In the worked example built from the city’s tables, the building permit comes to $1,405.48 and plan review to $801.12. The trade permits add $688.69 for electrical, $477.86 for plumbing and $393.53 for mechanical. The technology fee adds $150.67. Total: about $3,917 in permit and plan review fees for the house.

Compare that with the county’s $575 for a 2,000 sq ft home and you see the whole story of this post in one line. The same house, a few miles apart, can carry a permit bill six or seven times larger inside Raleigh than outside it. Neither number is wrong. They are just two governments pricing the same work two different ways.

Smaller Raleigh jobs have their own shape. PermitCalculator, working from the city’s published schedule, puts a bathroom remodel permit and a kitchen remodel permit at $397 each, and an HVAC permit at $129. Those are handy sanity checks: if your Raleigh quote for a bathroom permit is far above $397, ask what else is in it.

The fees that sit outside both schedules

Whichever office issues your permit, the permit fee is not the last government charge on a new home.

In Raleigh, a new meter brings Raleigh Water capital facility fees — the FY27 guide lists $2,223 for sewer and $1,447 for water with a new meter. Those two lines alone can rival the permit itself. Zoning, stormwater and other development fees can apply on top, depending on the lot.

That is how a Raleigh new home with about $3,917 in permit fees can still land near the $13,000 total government fee figure that BuildCostLookup estimates for a standard single-family home in Raleigh. The gap between the two numbers is not padding. It is the utility and development charges that sit outside the building permit.

In the county, the same lesson holds in a smaller way. A $575 county permit can still be followed by septic, well, driveway and zoning charges that depend on the lot. A lot that needs a new septic system will cost more to permit than a lot that ties into an existing one, and no fee table can tell you that until someone looks at your land.

One more Raleigh line is worth knowing before you start, not after. If you do work that needed a permit without getting one, the city charges an administrative fee equal to the permit fee on top of the permit itself. In plain terms, the permit costs double. Getting the permit first is always the cheaper path.

How to get your real number before you start

Start with the address, not the project. County or city — that one answer picks your fee schedule, and in Wake County it changes the bill more than the size of the house does.

If you are in unincorporated Wake County or one of the four contract towns, do the county math yourself: measure gross floor area the county way, multiply by $0.25, add $75. Then call the county office and ask what sits outside the building permit for your lot — septic, well, driveway and zoning are the usual four.

If you are in Raleigh, use the city’s fee calculator with your project type, and remember it gives an estimate. The portal invoice is the final figure. Budget the trade minimums, the plan review and the technology fee from the start, and ask early whether water and sewer capital fees apply to your lot.

Either way, write the estimate down next to your builder’s quote. If the builder carries a permit allowance in the contract, you now have a real schedule to check it against, line by line, instead of a guess.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Wake County and Raleigh?

In unincorporated Wake County, a new home permit is $75 plus $0.25 per square foot of gross floor area, all trades included — $575 for a 2,000 sq ft home. Inside Raleigh, the building permit is 0.38% of the city’s calculated value with a $124 minimum per trade, plus plan review and a 4% technology fee, so a new home totals about $3,917 in permit fees in the city’s worked example.

Why is a Raleigh permit so much more than a Wake County permit?

They are priced two different ways by two different offices. The county charges by size at a low flat rate and folds all trades into one fee. Raleigh charges by project value, prices each trade separately with its own minimum, and adds plan review and a technology fee on top.

Does the Wake County fee schedule apply inside Raleigh?

No. Wake County Permits and Inspections covers unincorporated Wake County and, by contract, Knightdale, Rolesville, Wendell and Zebulon. Inside Raleigh, the city’s Planning and Development Department issues the permit under the city’s own FY27 fee guide.

How does Wake County measure square footage for the permit fee?

By gross floor area: everything finished inside the inside perimeter of the exterior walls, with no deduction for the garage, corridors, stairs or closets. A garage inside that perimeter counts toward the fee, so measure the county way before you estimate.

What does a bathroom or kitchen permit cost in Raleigh?

PermitCalculator, working from the city’s published schedule, puts both a bathroom remodel permit and a kitchen remodel permit in Raleigh at $397, and an HVAC permit at $129. Your total can move if the job adds trades or hits the per-trade minimums more than once.

When do Raleigh permit fees change?

Raleigh runs on a July-to-June fiscal year and re-issues its Development Fee Guide each year. The FY27 guide took effect July 1, 2026 and raised the new-home permit rate to 0.38% of value. An estimate from last year’s guide is last year’s price.
