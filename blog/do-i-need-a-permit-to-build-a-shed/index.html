Do I need a permit to build a shed — and how big can it be without one?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 21, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

Do I need a permit to build a shed? Often no, if the shed stays small, one story, and plain storage. The model code, IRC Section R105.2, needs no building permit for a one-story detached shed up to 200 square feet. California cuts that line to 120 square feet, and Hillsborough County, Florida, cuts it to under 150 square feet. Your city sets the real line, and wiring, plumbing, or a permanent foundation can trigger a permit at any size.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Model code line: no building permit for a one-story detached storage shed up to 200 square feet (IRC R105.2).
  • California line: 120 square feet statewide, and Los Angeles County adds a 12-foot height cap and a 24-inch roof overhang cap.
  • Local lines move a lot: Hillsborough County under 150 square feet, Chicago 150 or less, Philadelphia 200 for the building permit but 130 for zoning in a rear yard, Wake County a 12-foot side rule.
  • Power, water, a permanent foundation, or living space in the shed can each trigger a permit, even in a tiny shed.
  • No building permit still means zoning rules apply: setbacks, height, and lot coverage can stop or move a shed that needs no permit at all.

Do I need a permit to build a shed where you live?

Start with the honest answer: it depends on your address, not on the shed kit you bought. There is no single national shed law. Each city and county adopts a building code, sets its own size line, and layers zoning rules on top.

Most places start from the same model code, so the pattern is easy to learn: a size line, a short list of conditions (one story, detached, storage use only), and a set of traps that pull a small shed back into the permit office — wiring, plumbing, a permanent foundation, or a floodplain lot.

One more thing. A permit exemption is narrow. It waives the building permit only, not zoning, not your HOA rules. Keep that split in mind: building permit in one column, every other rule in the other column.

The model code line is 200 square feet

Most U.S. cities and counties base their home building rules on the International Residential Code, the IRC. Its list of work that needs no permit, Section R105.2, starts with sheds. A one-story detached accessory structure used as a tool or storage shed, a playhouse, or a similar use needs no building permit if its floor area does not exceed 200 square feet.

Read the conditions as part of the number. One story means no second floor. Detached means it does not touch the house or the garage. Storage use means tools, bikes, and lawn gear, not a bedroom or a rental unit.

The 200 square foot line covers most sheds sold at home stores. A 10 by 16 shed is 160 square feet. A 12 by 16 shed is 192 square feet, still under the line. A 12 by 20 shed is 240 square feet, over the line. The code also says that skipping the permit does not grant a pass on the rest of the code. The exemption is about paperwork, not about standards.

California cuts the line to 120 square feet

California writes its own version of the code, and its shed line is lower: 120 square feet under California Building Code Section 105.2 and California Residential Code Section R105.2. A 10 by 12 shed sits exactly on that line. A 12 by 12 shed at 144 square feet needs a permit in California even though it passes with room to spare in a model-code state.

Los Angeles County adds conditions on top: the shed must also stay at or under 12 feet tall, with a roof projection of no more than 24 inches. Any electrical, plumbing, or mechanical gear in the shed needs its own permit, and every structure, permit or not, must meet zoning rules.

Five real places, five different lines

Here is where the national advice falls apart. No two of these five lines match.

In unincorporated Hillsborough County, Florida, residential sheds and greenhouses under 150 square feet need no building permit. The county words it as less than 150, so a shed at exactly 150 square feet is a question to ask the county, not a safe guess.

In Austin, Texas, the city keeps the model code’s 200 square feet and adds conditions. The shed must be one story and detached, no more than 15 feet tall, not a dwelling, with no plumbing, and not in a flood hazard area. Miss any one and the exemption is gone. A 180 square foot shed with a sink in it needs a permit in Austin. The same shed with no plumbing does not.

In Chicago, the Department of Buildings exemption list covers a shed, gazebo, or playhouse that is single story, 150 square feet or less in footprint, no more than 15 feet tall, with no plumbing. Chicago runs its own construction codes, not the IRC, so its line is a city choice.

In Philadelphia, the building permit line and the zoning line are two different numbers, and the zoning number bites first. The city’s shed guide from Licenses and Inspections says a shed for a one- or two-family home needs no building permit at 200 square feet or less, and no zoning permit either if it sits in the rear yard at 130 square feet or less, one story, and no more than 15 feet tall. The same shed in a front or side yard needs a zoning permit. A shed over 130 and up to 200 square feet is not allowed by the zoning code as a simple approval, so the city issues a refusal and your path runs through an appeal. Over 200 square feet, both permits are required. A 12 by 16 shed at 192 square feet passes the building line and still cannot go in by right. That is why you check both columns.

In Wake County, North Carolina, the rule is not square feet at all. An accessory building needs a building permit if any dimension is greater than 12 feet. A 12 by 12 shed at 144 square feet needs no building permit there, while a long, thin 8 by 16 shed at 128 square feet does, because one side runs past 12 feet.

Size is not the only test

A shed can be small and still need a permit.

Power is the most common one. Running electricity to a shed is electrical work, and electrical work needs its own permit in city after city, no matter how small the shed is. The exemption covers the box. It never covers the wiring, the pipes, or the ductwork you add to the box.

Water is the same story. A sink or a bathroom in the shed is plumbing work. Austin’s exemption drops away the moment the shed contains plumbing, and Chicago’s does too.

A permanent foundation can matter as much as size. In Hillsborough County, a shed set on a permanent foundation needs a permit even under the size line. Ask before you pour, because the slab is the hard part to undo.

How you use the shed matters too. The exemptions are written for storage and similar light uses. A shed someone sleeps in is a dwelling in the eyes of the code, and dwellings are never permit-free. If your real plan is a backyard office or a guest room, take the permit path from the start.

Where the shed sits can end the exemption as well. Austin names flood hazard areas in its rule. A shed on a utility easement can be ordered moved even if it needed no permit to build.

No building permit does not mean no rules

Say your shed passes every test above and needs no building permit. You are not done checking. Zoning rules still set where the shed can go: how far from your lot lines, how far from the house, how tall, and how much of your yard all your structures can cover. Those rules apply to exempt sheds in Los Angeles County, in Austin, and in Hillsborough County, because the exemption texts say so themselves.

Philadelphia shows the split at its sharpest. Its zoning code, not its building code, caps a by-right rear-yard shed at 130 square feet. Your shed can be legal to build and illegal to place.

Then come the private rules. An HOA can set its own shed limits, often tighter than the city’s, and no permit office will check those for you.

A short call solves most of this. Call your building department, give your address and shed size, and ask three things: your size line, your setback lines, and whether any trade work in your plan needs its own permit.

What a shed permit costs when you do need one

If your shed lands over the line, the permit itself is usually a modest line in the budget. The fee shape changes by city, so here are three real ones.

Hillsborough County prices a residential shed on a flat fee schedule. A shed, carport, deck, or screen room permit for a house costs $380. One flat line, not a per-foot math problem.

Chicago prices a permitted shed by formula, but the formula lands under the city’s floor for small work. A shed over 150 square feet is a plan-based permit, and the city’s 2026 minimum fee of $602 controls, paid as a $600 deposit at filing and the balance at issuance.

Los Angeles County prices by value. Its Table 1-A charges $98.09 for a job valued over $700 up to $25,000, plus $25.27 for each added $1,000 of value over the first $1,000, plus a $41.35 issuance fee. A small shed permit in the county lands in the low hundreds of dollars, before trade permits.

Two cost warnings. First, trade permits stack: a wired and plumbed shed can carry three permits, each with its own fee and inspection. Second, building first and asking later costs more. Hillsborough County adds a 100 percent penalty surcharge on top of the standard fee for work done without a permit.

How to get a shed permit, step by step

If your shed needs a permit, the path is short. Here is the order that works in most cities.

First, fix your size and your spot. Measure the shed’s outside floor area, pick the exact place it will sit, and measure the distance from that spot to each lot line and to the house.

Second, get a site plan. Most cities want a simple drawing of your lot showing the house, the shed, and the distances to the lot lines. Many take a hand drawing on a copy of your survey.

Third, file the application. Small sheds often go through an online portal: Hillsborough County takes applications through HillsGovHub, Philadelphia through eCLIPSE, and Chicago through its e-permit system. You describe the shed and pay the fee or the deposit.

Fourth, answer the review. A reviewer checks the site plan against setbacks and the shed against the code. If the reviewer sends corrections, fix the drawing and send it back. That is normal, not a denial.

Fifth, build to the paper and call for inspection. When the final inspection passes, your shed is on the record as legal work.

What happens if you build without one

People do skip this. Here is what the skip can cost.

The city can make you permit the shed after the fact, at a higher fee. Hillsborough County’s 100 percent surcharge is the pattern: the same permit, twice the price, plus your time.

The city can also make you move or remove the shed. That order usually comes from the placement rules, not the size rules: a shed too close to the lot line, on an easement, or over the lot coverage cap.

The problem follows you to a sale. An unpermitted shed that needed a permit shows up in buyer questions and permit history checks. A shed that never needed a permit is a non-issue at sale, which is one more reason to make the five-minute call before you build and keep a dated note of the answer.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to build a shed under 200 square feet?

In a model-code town, no building permit is needed for a one-story detached storage shed up to 200 square feet, under IRC Section R105.2. But California’s line is 120 square feet, Hillsborough County’s is under 150, and Chicago’s is 150 or less, so check your own city first. Zoning setbacks still apply at any size.

How big can a shed be without a permit in California?

Up to 120 square feet of floor area, one story and detached, under the California codes. Los Angeles County adds two more caps: no more than 12 feet tall and no more than 24 inches of roof projection. A 12 by 12 shed at 144 square feet needs a permit.

Does a shed on a concrete slab need a permit?

It can. In Hillsborough County, a shed on a permanent foundation needs a permit even if it sits under the 150 square foot line. Other cities treat a slab as part of the structure and review it with the shed. Ask your building department before you pour.

Can I put electricity in a shed without a permit?

No. The size exemption covers the shed itself, not the systems inside it. Electrical work needs an electrical permit even in a shed that needed no building permit, and the same holds for plumbing and mechanical work.

What happens if I build a shed without a permit when one was required?

Expect an after-the-fact permit at a penalty price, and possibly an order to move or remove the shed if it breaks setback or easement rules. Hillsborough County adds a 100 percent surcharge to the standard fee for work done without a permit. The issue also surfaces when you sell.

Do prefab or kit sheds follow the same rules as sheds I build myself?

Yes. The rules turn on the finished structure: its size, height, foundation, and any power or water, not on how it arrived in your yard. A kit shed over your city’s line needs the same permit as a site-built shed of the same size.
