How much does a building permit cost in Austin and Travis County?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 25, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

The building permit cost Austin TX homeowners pay depends on which office issues the permit. Inside the City of Austin, a 2,000 square foot new home runs about $2,345.83 all in under the city’s FY2025-26 fee schedule, effective October 1, 2025 — that total covers the application fee, plan review, and the five trade permits the city charges separately. Outside the city, in unincorporated Travis County, the county’s basic residential development permit is a flat $149 under the county schedule effective January 1, 2026, with separate fees for floodplain work, driveways, and septic systems. The city and the county are two different systems, and the first step is knowing which one covers your address.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Inside Austin, you pay three layers: an application fee, plan review at filing, and five trade permit fees at issuance.
  • A 2,000 sq ft new home in the city totals about $2,345.83 on the FY2025-26 schedule.
  • In unincorporated Travis County, the basic residential development permit is a flat $149 — the county does not run the same building-code permit the city does.
  • Extra county fees add up fast outside the city: $287 for a floodplain permit, $165 for a driveway permit, $712 for a septic permit.
  • Austin resets its fees every October 1, so an old quote can be a year out of date.

First question: city or county?

This is where most people get confused, and it is a fair confusion. Austin sits inside Travis County, so people assume one permit office covers both. It does not.

If your project is inside the Austin city limits, the City of Austin’s Development Services Department issues your building permit. The city runs a full building-code review: plans are checked, five trade permits are charged, and inspectors come out as the work moves along.

If your project is in unincorporated Travis County — outside any city’s limits — the county is your office, and the county’s job is different. Travis County does not issue building-code permits for houses the way the city does. The county charges development fees, mainly for floodplain and site work, and the city-style trade permits do not come from the county at all.

So the same house can carry two very different permit bills depending on which side of the city line the lot sits on. Before you budget anything, check the address. Everything below splits along that line.

Total City of Austin permit cost for a 2,000 sq ft new home on the FY2025-26 schedule: $106.72 application, $1,255.10 plan review, $984.01 across the five trade permits

How Austin charges: three layers, five trades

Austin does not give you one permit price. The city charges in layers, and if you only hear the first number, the rest feels like a surprise. It is not a surprise once you see the shape of it.

Layer one is the application processing fee. For a residential project it is $106.72 on the FY2025-26 schedule. You pay it when you file.

Layer two is plan review. The city charges for the review when you submit your application, and that fee is not refundable. Plan review is priced by project type and size. A new single-family home starts at $896.50 for the first 1,000 square feet and climbs from there. For the 2,000 square foot example, plan review comes to $1,255.10.

Layer three is the permit fees themselves, paid when the permit is issued. Austin splits these by trade, and there are five: Building, Electrical, Mechanical, Plumbing, and Energy. Each trade has its own table by square footage, so one house produces five permit fees. On the 2,000 square foot example, the five together come to $984.01.

Add the three layers and you get the $2,345.83 total. None of the layers is hidden — the city’s schedule prints them all — but no single city page adds them up for you. That is the gap this post fills.

Building permit cost Austin TX: what common city projects pay

The new-home example is the one people ask about most, but smaller projects follow the same three layers.

A 400 square foot home addition in the city totals about $1,842.04 on the same schedule: $106.72 application, $896.50 plan review, and $838.82 across the five trade permits. Notice that plan review barely drops even though the addition is much smaller than a whole house. Review work does not shrink as fast as the project does, and the fee shows it.

Smaller jobs use the city’s express and small-project routes. Bathroom work, foundation repair, and similar small jobs carry lower review fees — the express residential plan review fee is $136.45 on the FY2026 homeowner fee list the city published, with trade fees added per job. A pool, a carport, or a garage conversion goes through the small-projects route instead, with its own review fee on top of the trade fees.

The honest way to use these numbers: treat them as the shape of your bill, then run your exact square footage through the city’s schedule or the Austin Permit Estimator before you sign a contract. Two projects that sound alike can land in different size brackets, and the bracket edges are where the jumps sit.

The county side: unincorporated Travis County

Outside the city, the bill looks nothing like the city’s — because the county is charging for different things.

The county’s basic development permit for a residence, the “A” permit, is a flat $149 under the Travis County schedule effective January 1, 2026. There is no per-trade stack and no plan review fee on that basic permit. That low number surprises people who just read the city figures above, so read it for what it is: a development permit, not a city building permit with inspections by trade.

Then the add-ons. If the work sits in a special flood hazard area, the residential floodplain permit is $287 per application, and it replaces the basic $149 permit rather than stacking on top of it. A new driveway on a county road carries a $165 driveway permit. If the house needs a septic system, the private sewage permit runs $712 per application for a system up to 500 gallons per day.

A county lot that needs all three — floodplain, driveway, and septic — can pass $1,100 in county fees before any city or utility charge enters the picture. A plain lot on high ground with an existing driveway can stay at the flat $149. The lot decides the bill more than the house does.

One more county fee shows up in the guides and confuses people, so it is worth naming. The Travis County Fire Marshal charges its own building permit fees for the fire code work it reviews — PermitFlow’s Travis County guide lists $790 for a project valued under $200,000, rising by bracket above that. That is a fire code permit, a separate charge from the development permit above, and it applies to the projects the Fire Marshal reviews. If your project is one of those, budget for both offices.

Fees that sit outside both schedules

Whichever side of the line you build on, some costs sit outside the permit fee itself. Budget for them early so they do not land as surprises.

Utility work is the big one. A new home can need water and electric service work that neither the city permit fee nor the county development fee covers. Austin Energy charges its own application fees when a project adds square footage or needs a larger meter or panel — the city’s homeowner fee list puts those planning application fees at $200 each, plus a technology fee on top. Tap and connection charges, where they apply, are separate again.

Plans cost money too. Most permitted projects need drawings a reviewer can check. If you hire a designer or architect, that fee is yours, not the city’s, and it usually runs well past the permit fee itself.

Corrections cost time more than money. If the reviewer sends your plans back with comments, you fix and resubmit. The permit schedule has resubmittal and revision fees — the city’s FY2026 list puts a residential plan review resubmittal at $532.52 under the capped schedule — but the bigger cost is the weeks the project sits still. A complete first filing is the cheapest fee-saving move there is.

How to get your real number before you start

Start with the address. City or county — that one answer picks your fee schedule.

If you are in the city, run the Austin Permit Estimator with your project type and square footage. It works the city’s own FY2025-26 tables, all three layers, and gives you the total the tables produce. Then note the date. Austin adopts a new fee schedule every October 1, so an estimate run last fall is last year’s price.

If you are in the county, start from the $149 basic permit and add only what your lot actually needs: floodplain, driveway, septic. Check the floodplain question with the county map, not a guess — that one answer moves the bill by hundreds.

Either way, keep the estimate next to your contract. If a builder quotes you a permit allowance, you now have a real schedule to check it against, line by line.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Austin and Travis County?

In the City of Austin, a 2,000 sq ft new home totals about $2,345.83 on the FY2025-26 schedule — application, plan review, and five trade permits together. In unincorporated Travis County, the basic residential development permit is a flat $149, with floodplain, driveway, and septic fees added only when the lot needs them.

Why does Austin charge five separate trade permits?

The city’s fee schedule prices Building, Electrical, Mechanical, Plumbing, and Energy as five distinct permits, each with its own square-footage table. One house therefore produces five permit fees, paid at issuance, on top of the application fee and plan review paid at filing.

Does Travis County charge the same building permit fees as Austin?

No. Travis County does not run the city’s building-code permit for houses. The county charges development fees — a flat $149 basic residential permit on the schedule effective January 1, 2026 — plus site fees like $287 for floodplain work and $712 for a septic permit. The Fire Marshal’s fire code permit is a separate charge again.

When do Austin permit fees change?

Every October 1. Austin runs on an October-to-September fiscal year and re-adopts its Development Services fee schedule with each budget. The FY2025-26 schedule took effect October 1, 2025, under Ordinance No. 20250813-004.

Is the Austin plan review fee refundable if my project falls through?

No. The city charges plan review at application and the fee is non-refundable unless it was collected in error. That is worth knowing before you file on a project that is not fully decided yet.

What does a home addition permit cost in Austin?

A 400 sq ft addition totals about $1,842.04 on the FY2025-26 schedule: $106.72 application, $896.50 plan review, and $838.82 across the five trade permits. Your exact total depends on your square footage bracket, so run your own size through the city’s tables.
