How long does a commercial building permit take in Houston, Dallas, Austin, Phoenix and Chicago?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 6, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

It depends on which clock you mean. On the first-review clock, Houston and Phoenix work in weeks, Dallas aims for about 15 business days, Austin publishes 7 to 25 business days by type, and Chicago runs longest. The second clock is the one that bites: with correction rounds, a typical Texas commercial job takes about 6 to 16 weeks to an issued permit, and Chicago's own tracker put total time at 106 days on November 6, 2026. Bid to the second clock.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Two clocks matter: the first review, and the full run to an issued permit with corrections. They are not close.
  • Dallas posts the clearest target: about 15 business days for a first commercial review, 7 for a resubmittal.
  • Austin splits its clock by job type: new construction 25 business days, smaller work less, simple jobs as fast as a day.
  • Phoenix writes its clock into a state-law policy: 50, 70, or 90 calendar days of substantive review, by size.
  • Chicago is slowest here by every measure checked. Its tracker splits the wait: 40 days with the city, 66 with the applicant.

The two clocks, and why bids go wrong

Most arguments about permit times start the same way: two people quote two different numbers, and both of them are right.

The first clock is the review itself. It starts when a complete application lands, and stops when the city sends its first answer back. Cities publish it because it is the part they control. It's also the smallest number in the story.

The second clock runs from filing to the day the permit is in your hand. It includes every correction round, and the days the file sat on your own desk. That second part catches people off guard. In Chicago's tracker, the applicant held the file longer than the city did.

PermitPlace's 2026 study gives the national frame. Across 741 cities, the average first review was 22.9 days, and the middle city sat at 14 days. Texas was the fastest large state, at 17.4 days on average. A Texas job starts with a real edge, and you can burn it fast by filing sloppy plans.

Average first review of 22.9 days and a median of 14 days across 741 cities; Texas averaged 17.4 days across 81 cities, the fastest large state

How long does a building permit take in Houston?

Houston is fast for a city its size, in part because it has no general zoning code, so there is no zoning fight before plan review starts. The Permitting Center still checks building, fire, traffic, floodplain, and utility rules. Fast doesn't mean loose. It just means one less gate.

PermitPlace's Houston guide, updated in February 2026, puts a first review for new commercial construction at 15 to 37 business days. A tenant improvement or remodel runs 10 to 20 business days. Minor work that fits the One-Stop walk-in review can come back the same day or within 2 business days.

Then corrections start. Most Houston commercial projects go through two or three correction rounds, and each round adds a full review period. Add those in, and a typical Houston commercial permit takes 8 to 16 weeks to issuance. That is the number to put in a bid schedule. For a full commercial build, 8 to 16 weeks is the safer read.

First review of 15 to 37 business days for new commercial construction and 10 to 20 business days for a tenant improvement or remodel; 8 to 16 weeks in total once correction rounds count

How long does a building permit take in Dallas?

Dallas gives you the clearest posted target of the five. The city's Planning and Development Guide says review time for a commercial application runs about 15 business days.

The city's performance goals sharpen that picture. For 2025–26, Dallas set a goal of 15 business days for a first commercial review. A resubmitted review gets a goal of 7 business days. Its express route, the Q-Team, carries a goal of 10 business days, and the monthly figures in that report show express reviews landing in about 3 days.

PermitPlace's Dallas guide is wider, at 10 to 25 business days for a first review, with up to 10 business days of processing before that review starts. Put the two together and it's simple: a clean file can hit the city's 15-day aim, and a messy one drifts to the top of the guide's range. With correction rounds, the guide puts a typical Dallas job at 8 to 16 weeks in total.

City goal of 15 business days for a first commercial review and 7 business days for a resubmitted review; express Q-Team goal of 10 business days

Dallas commercial review goals — City of Dallas, Texas · Fiscal year 2025–26 performance goals, checked November 6, 2026

Source: City of Dallas, Planning and Development Department Performance Goals FY2025-2026 · Date pulled: November 6, 2026

Dallas files now run through DallasNow, launched May 5, 2025. And the city's old open-data feed ends in 2020, so a fast review clock does not mean a live public list exists.

How long does a building permit take in Austin?

Austin publishes the most detailed clock of the five, split by the kind of work. Its Commercial Plan Review page says a review can take 7 to 25 business days, depending on type. New construction gets 25 business days, new construction under 25,000 square feet gets 15, and a remodel gets 15. A correction round gets 15 business days for new construction, and 10 for a remodel.

Small work moves much faster. The Quick Turn-Around route can finish a simple review within one business day when a project qualifies, and small projects sit at 5 business days. An Austin finish-out and a ground-up building live on different clocks, and quoting one number for both is how schedules break.

PermitPlace's Austin guide lands in the same place: 15 to 25 business days for a first review, about 10 business days per correction round, and 6 to 12 weeks in total for a typical project. Site, drainage, and environmental reviews sit outside that clock. If your site needs them, your real wait is longer, and your estimator should price that risk out in the open.

7 to 25 business days for a commercial review by application type; 25 business days for new construction, 15 for new construction under 25,000 square feet and for remodels

How long does a building permit take in Phoenix?

Phoenix does something the other four do not: it writes its clock into a policy tied to state law. Arizona's licensing-time law, A.R.S. 9-835, makes the city publish the time it gets to review an application, in calendar days. The policy splits that clock into an administrative step and a substantive step, which covers the first full review and one later review.

For commercial and multifamily review, the policy gives 10 calendar days for the administrative step, then 50 calendar days of substantive review for a minor project, 70 for a medium one, and 90 for a major one. Minor means under 5,000 square feet and under $350,000. Major means over 50,000 square feet or over $5,000,000. So a big Phoenix project gets a legal review window of about three months before anyone calls it late.

Administrative review of 10 calendar days, plus substantive review of 50 calendar days for a minor commercial project, 70 for a medium one, and 90 for a major one

Phoenix commercial review frames — City of Phoenix, Arizona · City license-time policy, September 2025 version, checked November 6, 2026

Source: City of Phoenix, License Time Frames, A.R.S. 9-835 Policy · Date pulled: November 6, 2026

In practice, a first review often lands sooner. PermitPlace's general table puts Phoenix at 10 to 15 business days for a first review and 2 to 4 weeks in total for a typical project. Hold on to both numbers: the guide shows what a normal job sees, and the policy shows the most time the city may take.

Phoenix also has a true fast lane. Under the Self-Certification Program, a city-trained registered professional can certify code compliance in place of a city review, and permits can issue within 1 to 5 business days. Some projects cannot use it, and the professional carries real responsibility. For a qualified team with clean plans, it is the fastest legal route here.

How long does a building permit take in Chicago?

Chicago is the slow city here, and it's not close. PermitPlace's 2026 study calls it the slowest large metro in the country, at 92 days for a first review. That study works from published guidelines for typical and simple commercial projects, and warns that complex work takes much longer.

The city publishes a live tracker that is blunter than any guide. On November 6, 2026, its Time to Permit page put total time at 106 days: 40 days of processing with the Department of Buildings, and 66 days with the applicant. Read that split twice. The biggest block of waiting, on the city's own count, was the file sitting with the project team.

The normal Chicago route for work that needs plans is Standard Plan Review. PermitPlace's Chicago team says permits on that route typically take 6 to 8 weeks, while smaller projects may take a few days. The city also runs an Accelerated Plan Review, and a self-certification route where a certified architect or engineer takes responsibility for compliance. If your job can use a faster route, that choice belongs at the start, not after the first correction letter lands.

How do you shorten the wait in any of these cities?

You can take back the days that are yours.

File a complete set. Every city guide in the sources names the same top cause of delay: an application that arrives missing pieces. A missing sheet does not cost a day. It costs a full review round.

Answer corrections as one package. Chicago's tracker shows why: 66 of its 106 days sat with the applicant. Hold the file until every comment has an answer, then send it back whole.

Ask about the fast lane before you file. Austin has Quick Turn-Around, Dallas has the Q-Team, Phoenix has self-certification, Chicago has accelerated review, and Houston has One-Stop review for minor work. None of them help a job that filed on the standard route last month. The lane is chosen at filing.

Price the wait in the bid. PermitPlace's 2026 study puts the carrying cost of a delayed commercial project at about $500 a day. Even at half that rate, a six-week slip costs real money. A bid that names the permit window, city by city, reads as honest. One that promises a start date no city can meet doesn't.

And if the wait itself is the problem, that's the job an expediter gets paid for. Our guide to what a permit expediter costs works through those prices in full.

Frequently asked questions

Which of these five cities is fastest for a commercial building permit?

Going by published first reviews, Houston, Dallas, and Phoenix sit at the fast end, in weeks. Dallas posts the clearest single target, about 15 business days for a first review. Chicago is slowest by both measures we checked: 92 days for a first review in PermitPlace's 2026 study, and 106 days in total on the city's own tracker.

Is the first review the same as getting the permit?

No. A first review is just the city's first answer, and it's often a list of corrections. The permit comes after your team fixes the plans and the city accepts them. Most commercial projects in the Texas guides go through two or three correction rounds, and each round adds its own review time.

Why does Chicago take so much longer?

The sources point to how the review is set up. Chicago runs a multi-department review, and PermitPlace calls it the slowest large metro in its 2026 study. Its tracker adds a second fact: 66 of the 106 total days were time with the applicant, not the city.

Can I pay to make a commercial permit faster?

In several cities, yes — but only in set ways. Dallas sells express Q-Team time, Phoenix lets a certified professional self-certify a project so a permit can issue in 1 to 5 business days, and Chicago runs an Accelerated Plan Review. Austin's fast routes are for small, simple work. Check your project against the rules before you count on one.

How long should I put in my bid for a Houston commercial permit?

Use the total, not the first review. PermitPlace's Houston guide puts a first review at 15 to 37 business days, and the total at 8 to 16 weeks once corrections count. Bid the longer figure, and treat anything faster as a bonus.
