How do I find pool, fence and deck permits in Travis County before my competitors do?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 6, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

Start with the building permits, not the trade permits. In October 2026, the City of Austin issued 147 building permits that name a pool, a fence, or a deck: 103 name a pool, 55 name a deck, and only 5 are fence-only permits. Austin pool permits are the deep list here — 1,076 pool building permits in the last 12 months. You can read the same records free in the city’s own data and in the PermitsByCounty Travis County report. Paid services charge $297 to $497 a month to watch this same list for you (PermitLeads ATX, checked November 4, 2026).

Austin pool permits: the October 2026 count

October was a strong month. Austin issued 1,165 building permits in total, and 147 of them name a pool, a fence, or a deck in the work description.

Pools did most of that. There were 103 pool building permits in October, up from 75 in September. Decks came next with 55. Fence-only permits were 5. Sixteen permits name both a pool and a deck — a new pool with concrete decking is one job filed on one permit — so the three counts overlap a little, and the honest total is 147 separate permits, not 163.

September’s combined count was 104, so October ran about 40% busier. The longer view is steady too: 1,501 building permits naming a pool, a fence, or a deck in the last 12 months — roughly 125 a month in one county.

Pools carry the list

A pool permit is rarely one crew’s job. Digging, plumbing, steel, the shell, electrical, decking, and the safety barrier all follow the same permit, in that order. That’s why pool builders watch this list every day — and why deck and fence crews should watch it too.

October’s pool permits were almost all backyard work: 97 residential and 6 commercial. Two names pulled far more than anyone else. Cody Pools and Paradisa Homes each pulled 11 pool building permits in October. Aquamarine Pools of Texas pulled 5, and Golden Hour Pools pulled 4. Across the full 12 months, Cody Pools leads with 89 pool building permits and Denali Pools follows with 74. A fence or deck sub who wants steady work could do worse than know those four office managers by name.

The work clusters. ZIP 78704 took 20 of October’s pool permits. ZIP 78703 took 14, and 78757 took 12. South-central and central Austin hold close to half the month’s pools, close enough together that one crew can bid several in a day.

One more detail matters if you build fences or gates. Ten of October’s pool permits name the enclosure, barrier, fence, or gate right in the pool’s own description. That’s required work — a new pool in Austin needs a barrier that passes its own check — and it never shows up as a fence permit. Read the pool list and you are reading a fence list too.

Decks: two kinds of work on one list

Austin issued 55 building permits naming a deck in October, up from 38 in September, and 583 across the last 12 months. Almost all of them — 53 of 55 — were residential.

Read them with one split in mind. Thirty-six of October’s 55 sit outside new-home construction: a covered deck added to a house, a backyard deck replaced on the same footprint, a deck built with a remodel. Those are jobs a homeowner or a remodeler hires for directly, and the permit names the project you can bid.

The rest ride inside new-house permits — an uncovered wood deck listed as one line in a new build. Nobody hires a deck crew off that line by itself. The call there goes to the builder on the permit, and the pitch is your rate for the next twenty houses, not this one deck. ATX Development and Catalyst ATX each show 3 deck-naming permits in October.

ZIP 78704 leads decks too, with 13 in October. After that the map spreads out: 78731 with 5, then 78722, 78744, 78745, and 78757 with 4 each.

A small reading warning, because it changes the count. Roofers use the word deck for the boards under shingles, and Austin’s records carry reroof permits that mention roof decking. Those are not deck jobs, and they are not in the 55. Neither are the two pool permits that say “no decking” in plain words.

Fences: the short list that misleads people

Five. That’s the whole fence-only list for October 2026, and the 12-month count is 51. A fence contractor who judged Austin by that number would walk away from a busy market. The number is small because of the rule, not the work.

Most fences in Austin need no permit at all. Under the City of Austin fencing regulations, a fence needs a permit only if it sits in a floodplain, stands more than 7 feet high at any point, or runs more than 6 feet high along a public right-of-way that is not an alley — and that last case also needs a city letter of authorization. A standard 6-foot backyard fence files nothing. Checked November 4, 2026.

October’s five match that shape: two 8-foot fences, a 6-foot replacement that ran about 240 linear feet with a double gate, and two vehicular gate jobs at multifamily sites. Tall, long, or motorized — that’s what gets filed.

So where is the rest of the fence work? Three places. First, inside pool permits, as the barrier work noted above. Second, inside new-home permits, where the yard fence is part of the build and never gets its own line. Third, in work that was never permitted because it never had to be — no permit list, paid or free, can show you that part. The list finds the permitted fence jobs and the pool barriers. It does not find plain privacy-fence replacements, and anyone who tells you it does is guessing.

So the crews paying $497 see the best Austin jobs a week before the crews paying $297 — and both see them on a Monday rhythm, days after the city issued the permit.

How to work the Travis County list, step by step

Step 1: Look daily, and look at building permits only. Open the Travis County report or the city’s issued-permits data each morning and filter to Building Permits issued in the last day or two. Skip the electrical, plumbing, and mechanical permits at this stage — they name the same jobs again.

Step 2: Search the descriptions for your trade’s word. Pool, deck, fence. Read the hits before you count them: a reroof that mentions roof decking is not a deck job.

Step 3: Split the deck and fence hits by who hires. A standalone deck or a remodel deck is a direct bid. A deck inside a new-home permit is a builder account — pitch the builder your per-house rate. The same split works for gates: a multifamily gate job hires a contractor directly.

Step 4: Sort by ZIP before you sort by date. October’s pools sat in 78704, 78703, and 78757 more than anywhere else. A tight route means more bids per day and a faster yes, because you can be on site the same afternoon.

Step 5: Call the name on the permit. For pool work, that usually means the pool builder — Cody Pools, Paradisa Homes, and Aquamarine Pools pulled 27 pool permits between them in October. Builders who pull pools every month need barrier, decking, and equipment subs they can count on. Introduce yourself as that, with your insurance and two recent jobs, not as a cold bid.

Step 6: Put the Monday services a week behind you. Note each permit’s issue date as you work. If your call lands within two days of the issue date, you called before the Monday dashboards went out — and a week before the Starter-plan crews saw the high-value ones.

Mistakes that waste this list

Don’t count every permit type. Austin filed 232 permits of all types that name a pool in October, but they cover 103 pool projects. The extra records are the same pools’ building, electrical, and plumbing permits counted again. Work from the 103.

Don’t wait for fence permits to find fence work. Five permits a month is the permitted tip of it. The barrier lines inside pool permits are the better fence list, and builders’ yard fences never appear at all.

Don’t quote Austin permit values. Most Austin permits state a value of $0 or $1, so a dollar total built from them is arithmetic on placeholders. Counts, ZIP codes, and contractor names are the honest numbers in Austin’s records. If you need to see how the same three trades look in a metro with real stated values, our Texas pool index covers Fort Worth’s figures.

Don’t pay for speed you can beat for free. A Monday delivery of records the city posted the week before is tidy, not early. Daily checking is early.

Frequently asked questions

How many pool permits did Austin issue in October 2026?

The City of Austin issued 103 building permits naming a pool in October 2026 — 97 residential and 6 commercial. Counting every permit type that mentions a pool, including the separate electrical and plumbing permits for the same jobs, gives 232 records for those same 103 projects.

Why are there so few fence permits in Austin?

Because most fences need no permit. Austin requires one only for a fence in a floodplain, a fence over 7 feet high at any point, or a fence over 6 feet high along a public right-of-way that is not an alley. Standard backyard fences file nothing, and pool barrier work is filed inside the pool permit.

How do I find deck permits in Travis County?

Filter Austin’s issued building permits to Building Permits and search the work descriptions for “deck.” In October 2026 that gives 55 permits. Read each hit: reroof permits mention roof decking, and those are roofing jobs, not deck jobs.

How much does it cost to get Austin pool permit leads?

PermitLeads ATX charges $297 a month for Starter and $497 a month for Pro, checked November 4, 2026. The underlying records are free: the city’s issued-permit data and the PermitsByCounty Travis County report carry the same permits, and the free weekly digest emails the week’s new ones.

When should I call about a new pool permit?

Within the first few days after it issues. Permits are filed 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks, so an early call reaches the builder while the crew schedule is still open. The excavation and plumbing calls belong first; decking and barrier calls land a few weeks later.

Does a permit record include the homeowner’s phone number?

No. An Austin permit record names the property, the work, and the contractor of record — not a homeowner’s phone or email. PermitLeads ATX lists owner phone and email as “coming soon” on its own site. PermitsByCounty publishes business permit facts only.
