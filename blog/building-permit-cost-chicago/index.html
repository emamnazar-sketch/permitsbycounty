How much does a building permit cost in Chicago and Cook County?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 27, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

The building permit cost Chicago projects pay depends on which road your job takes. Plan-based permits, the kind that need drawings and a review, carry a $602 minimum fee in 2026, and you pay a $600 non-refundable deposit when you file. The full fee is worked out from a formula: a construction factor times a scope factor times the floor area of the work. Small jobs that fit the city’s Express Permit Program skip that formula and pay flat fees instead — PermitCalculator puts a routine Chicago bathroom or kitchen permit at $325. Chicago permits come from the City of Chicago Department of Buildings. Cook County only issues permits for unincorporated areas, under its own schedule, with a $100 deposit for a home project.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Plan-based work in Chicago has a $602 minimum permit fee in 2026, with a $600 non-refundable deposit due when you file.
  • The plan-based fee is a formula, not a flat rate: construction factor times scope factor times floor area, basement included.
  • New home construction in Chicago carries a $2,450 minimum. Adding a dwelling unit carries a $1,800 minimum.
  • Express Permit jobs pay flat fees. A routine bathroom or kitchen permit is $325 on PermitCalculator, and most roof replacement permits issued in 2026 paid $450.
  • Cook County is a separate counter. It covers unincorporated areas only, takes a $100 home-project deposit, and prices new work by building volume.

First question: Chicago or Cook County?

Chicago sits inside Cook County, so a lot of people start at the county. That start costs them a trip.

If your address is inside the City of Chicago, your permit comes from the city’s Department of Buildings. The city runs the review, sets the fee, and sends the inspector. Cook County cannot issue your permit, and the county fee schedule does not apply to you.

If your address is in unincorporated Cook County — land that is not inside any city or village — the Cook County Department of Building and Zoning is your permit office. It takes e-permit applications online and prices work under the county schedule.

If your address is in a suburb, that suburb runs its own building department. Evanston, Oak Park, Skokie and every other municipality in Cook County set their own fees. The Chicago numbers and the county numbers in this post both stop at their borders.

So check the address first. That one check picks your office, your form, and your fee schedule. PermitsByCounty groups its Chicago records under Cook County (Chicago) because that is how people search for them. The records are City of Chicago records.

How Chicago prices a plan-based permit

Most bigger jobs — a new house, an addition, a gut rehab, work that moves walls — go through plan review. For those jobs Chicago does not use a price tag. It uses math.

The fee is the construction factor times the scope of review factor times the gross floor area of all the work. The construction factor comes from a city table and turns on what the building is used for and how it is built. The scope factor turns on the kind of work: new construction counts more than an alteration, and an alteration counts more than a repair. The area is the floor area of the work, and it includes basement floor area.

One worked line from the 2026 tables shows how this plays out. A new one-to-three-unit home built in masonry, Group R-5, Type III, is priced at $0.3675 per square foot, with a $2,450 minimum. The same home in wood-frame Type V construction is $0.33 per square foot, still with the $2,450 minimum. An addition to a one-to-three-unit home in Type V is also $0.33 per square foot, with a $900 minimum. A Level 2 or 3 alteration in a one-to-three-unit home in Type V is $0.22 per square foot, with a $600 minimum — and the citywide $602 floor sits on top of that.

For a small project, the formula almost never wins. The minimum wins. A 400 square foot alteration at $0.22 per square foot comes to $88 on paper, and the owner still pays the $602 minimum. That is why Chicago is the city everyone quotes at $602: for plan-based work, the floor is the fee for most home-sized jobs.

The deposit, and the penalty next to it

Plan-based applications in Chicago start with a deposit. From January 6, 2026, that deposit is $600. It is non-refundable, and it is credited toward your permit fee, so on a job that reaches issuance it is part of the fee, not an extra charge on top of it.

Read the two penalties that sit next to it before you file. If the Department of Buildings decides your application is substantially incomplete or otherwise deficient, it charges a penalty equal to the deposit — another $600. If it decides your application is ineligible for the review program you filed under, it charges the same $600 penalty again. A sloppy filing can put $1,200 at risk before review even starts: the deposit plus one penalty.

The practical lesson is plain. File complete drawings, file under the right program, and check the city’s own Permit Fee Calculator before you submit. The calculator runs the same tables the city uses, so the number it gives you is the number to budget around.

Two smaller 2026 lines are worth knowing. The minimum fee for a permit that adds a dwelling unit is $1,800, and from 2026 the minimum for a permit that removes dwelling units — a deconversion — rises to match it at $1,800. If your project changes the unit count either way, that floor applies.

Building permit cost Chicago: the Express road

Not every job needs drawings and a full review. Chicago’s Express Permit Program covers a long list of smaller, standard jobs, and those jobs pay flat fees from a stand-alone table instead of the formula. No $602 floor, no deposit math — you pay the listed fee for the listed job.

This is the road most home repair permits take. City permit records for permits issued in 2026 show most roof replacement permits paid $450. Roof recovers and repairs most often paid $175. Window and door replacements paid $175. A porch or deck permit paid $300. Many of these permits also carried a $25 fee, and masonry, solar, garage and interior jobs often carried a $50 zoning fee on top.

PermitCalculator, working from the city’s published schedule, puts a routine bathroom remodel permit at $325 and a routine kitchen permit at $325. Its note on the bathroom is the key one: a standard bathroom that does not touch load-bearing walls usually fits the Express road, where an interior alteration up to 500 square feet is a flat fee. The same bathroom with walls moving becomes a plan-based job, and the $602 floor takes over.

One fee went down in 2026, and it is worth naming because fee cuts are rare. The Express fee for a non-masonry fence up to 6 feet was cut from $150 to $75, effective mid-March 2026. A fence over 6 feet, or any masonry wall, is $300 instead, and a fence up to 5 feet that fits zoning needs no permit at all.

If your job is on the Express list, your permit cost is knowable to the dollar before you file. If it is not, budget from the $602 floor up, and run the city’s calculator with your real floor area.

What a new house or an ADU pays in Chicago

New construction sits at the top of the Chicago schedule. A new one-to-three-unit home pays the per-square-foot rate for its construction type — $0.3675 for masonry Type III, $0.33 for wood-frame Type V — and no new home permit in that class goes out for less than $2,450.

Additions pay less at the door. An addition to a one-to-three-unit home in Type V construction is $0.33 per square foot with a $900 minimum. On a 300 square foot addition the formula gives $99, so the $900 minimum is the real fee.

Accessory dwelling units have their own recent history in Chicago, and Post #55 on this site covers the ADU rules in full. On fees, an ADU permit that adds a dwelling unit carries the $1,800 minimum, plus a zoning review fee on most permits. Permit records from 2026 put a new coach house permit at $2,450 at the low end, because the new-construction minimum governs a separate new building.

Budget a new-build project in Chicago from the minimum up, not from the formula down. Until your floor area is large, the minimum is your fee.

The Cook County schedule, for unincorporated addresses

Unincorporated Cook County prices work a different way. The county does not use Chicago’s factors, and its numbers are lower at the door.

Every electronic filing starts with a deposit: $100 for a residential project or $500 for a non-residential project, paid online. The deposit comes off your final permit fee. It is not an extra charge.

New construction is priced by building volume: a $73.50 minimum, or $37.50 per 1,000 cubic feet up to 50,000 cubic feet, and $40.00 per 1,000 cubic feet over that. A private garage or shed carries a $73.50 minimum. Alterations and remodeling carry a $73.50 minimum plus $18.50 for each $1,000 of estimated cost over $5,000. Demolition of a single-family detached house is $250 flat.

Run one example. A $25,000 remodel in unincorporated Cook County pays $73.50 plus twenty times $18.50, so $443.50 for the building permit. The same remodel inside Chicago, if it needs plan review, starts at the $602 floor. Same county name on the map, two different bills — which is why the address check comes first.

Trade permits are separate at the county too. A plumbing permit starts at $143.60, which covers up to five fixtures, then $20 for each fixture over five.

A county permit is valid for 12 months from issue. If the work is not finished in that time, the permit becomes invalid until you take out an extension at 10% of the original permit cost or $25, whichever is greater.

How to get your real number before you start

Start with the address. Chicago, unincorporated county, or a suburb — that answer picks your schedule before any math matters. Cook County’s CookViewer map can tell you if a parcel is unincorporated.

If you are in Chicago, decide which road your job takes. On the Express list, your fee is the flat fee for that job, plus a $25 review fee or a $50 zoning fee where they apply. Off the list, run the city’s Permit Fee Calculator with your floor area, your occupancy, and your construction type, then check the answer against the floor that applies to you: $602 for most plan-based work, $900 for a small addition, $1,800 if the unit count changes, $2,450 for a new home.

Have the $600 deposit ready if you file a plan-based application, and file a complete set. The incomplete-application penalty is the same $600 again, and it buys you nothing.

If you are in unincorporated Cook County, price the building permit from the volume or alteration lines, add the trade permits your job touches, and have the $100 or $500 deposit ready for the online filing.

Write the fee down next to your contractor’s quote. If the contract carries a permit allowance, you now have the city’s or the county’s own schedule to check it against, instead of a guess.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a building permit cost in Chicago and Cook County?

Inside Chicago, a plan-based permit costs at least $602 in 2026, with a $600 deposit due at filing, and new home construction carries a $2,450 minimum. Express Permit jobs pay flat fees — a routine bathroom or kitchen permit is $325. In unincorporated Cook County, a remodel pays a $73.50 minimum plus $18.50 per $1,000 of cost over $5,000, with a $100 home-project deposit.

Does Cook County issue building permits for Chicago addresses?

No. The City of Chicago Department of Buildings issues permits inside Chicago. Cook County’s Department of Building and Zoning issues permits only for unincorporated areas and the Forest Preserves. Suburbs run their own building departments. PermitsByCounty files its Chicago records under Cook County so they are easy to find.

What is the $600 deposit for a Chicago building permit?

It is the non-refundable deposit due when you file a plan-based permit application, effective January 6, 2026. It is credited toward your permit fee. The city also charges a $600 penalty if your application is substantially incomplete, and a $600 penalty if you filed under a review program your project is not eligible for.

How is a Chicago plan-based permit fee calculated?

The city multiplies a construction factor by a scope of review factor by the gross floor area of the work, including basement area. The factors come from the city’s fee tables and turn on the building’s use, its construction type, and the kind of work. Minimum fees then apply — $602 for most plan-based work and $2,450 for new one-to-three-unit homes.

What does a bathroom or kitchen permit cost in Chicago?

PermitCalculator puts a routine bathroom remodel permit at $325 and a routine kitchen permit at $325, for jobs that fit the Express Permit Program and do not move load-bearing walls. A bathroom or kitchen that moves walls goes plan-based, and the $602 minimum takes over.

How long is a Cook County building permit valid?

An unincorporated Cook County permit is valid for 12 months from the date it is issued. If the work is not complete by then, the permit becomes invalid until you take out an extension, which costs 10% of the original permit cost or $25, whichever is greater.
