How do I renew or revive an expired building permit?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published October 30, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

Stop work first — work on an expired permit is work without a permit. Then ask the building department which path fits: most places let you renew or reactivate a recently expired permit if the plans have not changed, often for half the original fee, but the window is short. Under the model code most cities use, a permit expires after 180 days with no start or no approved inspection, per Section 105.5, and places like Maricopa County will not renew one expired for more than 360 days, checked October 30, 2026.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Extension, renewal, and a new permit are different things. An extension is asked for before expiry. A renewal is asked for after. A new permit starts over.
  • The clock is usually 180 days. No start, or no approved inspection, in 180 days and the permit expires in most places.
  • Act while the lapse is short. A recent lapse with no plan changes is often renewed for a small fee or half the original fee. A long lapse means full fees and current codes.
  • Stop work until the permit is active again. Work done while it is expired can be treated as work with no permit.
  • Finish the job this time. Pass the remaining inspections and get the permit closed.

Extension, renewal, or new permit — know which one you need

People use these words as if they were the same. Building departments do not.

An extension keeps a live permit live. You ask in writing before expiration, give a real reason, and the office adds time. If your permit is still active, ask for this first — it is the cheapest path here.

A renewal, reactivation, or re-open brings an expired permit back, usually for a set number of days, if you are inside its window and the work has not changed.

A new permit is a fresh start: new application, new review, full fees, and the current code. Some lapses leave no other choice. If the work is already done, a close-out may fit better — Florida’s path is near the end.

When does a building permit actually expire?

Most permits expire because time passed with no progress the department can see.

Section 105.5 of the 2018 International Building Code, adopted by Maricopa County, says permits expire 180 days from the date issued or the last completed inspection

The 180-day rule — Maricopa County, Arizona · County page checked October 30, 2026

Source: Maricopa County — Expired Permits · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

LegalClarity, checked October 30, 2026, puts the Florida rule plainly: a permit becomes invalid if work does not commence within 180 days, or if work is suspended or abandoned for 180 days after it begins. Each approved inspection resets the clock.

A job can look busy and still expire. Work the inspector never saw does not move the date. Close to 180 days, call for an inspection or ask for an extension in writing.

Stop work, then check three facts on the record

Do not keep building while you sort this out. Work after expiration is unpermitted work, and it can bring a stop-work order, higher fees, and orders to open finished work. Our guide on stop-work orders sets out what happens next.

Pull the record and write down three facts. First, the status word: active, expired, void, closed, abandoned, and withdrawn do not mean the same thing, and some cannot be revived. Second, how long it has been expired, in days — every fee and cutoff here turns on that number. Third, what is left: only the final, several open inspections, or no work started. Offices price those cases differently, as the Pasco County fees below show.

How to renew or revive an expired permit, step by step

1. Ask the department which path your permit qualifies for. Use the exact words from your record, and ask plainly if it is already void. Austin, one of our live metros, lets the owner or the contractor listed on the permit request reactivation, through the Austin Build + Connect portal if that account is assigned to it, or by a web form if not, per the City of Austin Modify a Permit page, checked October 30, 2026. That page also warns the owner is responsible for expired permits on the property, even if they did not own it when it was issued.

2. Put the request in writing, with a real reason. Almost every office here wants justifiable cause — a material delay, weather, a contractor change. Plano, Texas asks the contractor for a letter setting out why the permit expired, sent to the Assistant or Chief Building Official or the Permit Services Supervisor.

3. Say clearly that the plans and scope have not changed. Renewal is for the same job on the same plans. Changed work usually needs a revision or a new permit.

4. Pay the fee and note the new deadline. The new clock is 90 days in some places, 180 in most. Write that date down.

5. Keep the plans on site and call inspections early. A revived permit with no inspection in its new window expires again, and several places will not revive it a second time.

6. Finish and close it. Pass the final and make sure the record shows the permit closed. A permit left open can surface at a sale, as our guide on closing an open permit before selling explains.

What does renewal cost? Real fees from real offices

There is no national fee. Local fees rise with time.

A permit may be renewed within 360 days of expiration by completing another application and paying half the building permit fee, for another 180 days; a permit expired over 360 days cannot be renewed; one free 180-day extension is allowed on prior written request

Renewal within a year in Maricopa County — Maricopa County, Arizona · County page checked October 30, 2026

Source: Maricopa County — Expired Permits · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

An expired permit is renewed for one-half the building permit fee, if no changes were made to the approved plans and abandonment has not exceeded six months; it may be renewed only one time, for 180 days; an extension while still active carries no fee

One renewal only in Plano — Plano, Texas · Handout dated March 7, 2023, checked October 30, 2026

Source: City of Plano — Renewal of Expired Permit Process · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

Extensions cost $45 before expiration and run 90 days; expired permits with only finals or one inspection left cost $45 per open trade; expired permits with multiple open inspections are charged all original fees again; re-opens run 180 days; closed, abandoned, or withdrawn permits cannot be re-opened

Small fixed fees in Pasco County — Pasco County, Florida · Form revised June 16, 2025, checked October 30, 2026

Source: Pasco County — Permit Re-Open / Extension Request · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

Reinstatement 0 to 45 days past expiration costs the technology fee only; 46 to 90 days adds the application or trade fee; 91 days or more adds the permit fee; active permits may be extended up to 90 days before expiration

Fees that climb in Georgetown — Georgetown, Texas · City page checked October 30, 2026

Source: City of Georgetown — Expired Permit · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

LegalClarity’s Florida guide, checked October 30, 2026, gives the wider range: reactivation often needs a new application and fees from 50% to 100% of the original fee, depending on how long it has been expired. Early is cheap. Late is full price.

When a permit can no longer be renewed

Every window closes.

If no inspection is approved within 180 days after issuance, the permit expires; if it is not reactivated within 180 days of expiration, it is void and a new permit is required; reactivation needs no scope change, a written request within 180 days, and may be done only once

Expired becomes void in Portland — Portland, Oregon · City Code 27.03.030, checked October 30, 2026

Source: City of Portland — 27.03.030 Validity and Length of Permit · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

Maricopa County draws its line at 360 days. Georgetown draws an earlier one: over 90 days past expiration, a new application, all fees, and compliance with current codes are required, and the original permit is closed out, per its page checked October 30, 2026. Pasco County will not re-open a closed, abandoned, or withdrawn permit at all.

A new permit also means review under the current code, so changed rules can force a redesign. In Plano, 10 working days after expiration, the contractor’s registration is placed on hold until the permits are active again, per the handout above.

If the work is already done, you may need a close-out, not a renewal

Do not revive a permit just to close a finished job if your state gives a direct close-out path. Florida does.

If an expired permit’s requirements have been substantially completed, as determined by the local agency, it may be closed without a new permit, under the code in effect when the agency received the application; an agency may also close a permit 6 years after issuance, even without a final, if no safety hazard exists

Closing a substantially finished permit in Florida — Florida · 2023 Florida Building Code, Residential, Section 105.5, checked October 30, 2026

Source: 2023 Florida Building Code, Residential — 105.5, under Florida Statute 553.79(15) · Date pulled: October 30, 2026

The same section lets the owner keep the original contractor, hire a different licensed contractor to close the permit — liable only for work they do — or step in as owner-builder. LegalClarity’s Florida guide adds the covered-work route: an Affidavit Inspection Report from a Florida-registered architect or engineer, or re-opening for a city inspection, which could mean uncovering finished work. Ask which case you are in before you pay.

Austin example: two different doors

Austin separates the two doors cleanly. For a permit still active, it allows one extension per permit. If the site cannot be inspected in the normal rhythm, you can schedule a #114 Continuance of Work Inspection: if the inspector finds progress, the date is extended 180 days. For an expired permit, Austin calls it reactivation and charges fees issued after you submit. See the City of Austin Modify a Permit page, checked October 30, 2026.

Common mistakes that make a lapse worse

Working through the lapse. Work done in the gap is the hardest part to defend when the date comes to light.

Calling for an inspection to revive a dead permit. An inspection resets the clock on a live permit; in many places it does nothing for an expired one. Revive first, then inspect.

Changing the job while it is lapsed and saying nothing. Renewal is built on no change, and a silent change can void the renewal you paid for.

Letting the named contractor drift away. If that firm closed or will not sign, tell the office first. Pasco County requires the re-open request to be submitted by the contractor and signed by the license holder or an authorised signer on file, per its form above.

Reviving the permit and letting it lapse again. Several places allow only one renewal. Treat the new date as a hard finish line.

Frequently asked questions

How do I renew or revive an expired building permit?

Stop work, pull the record, and ask for a renewal or reactivation inside its window. File the written request with a real reason, state the plans have not changed, pay the fee, and schedule inspections in the new window.

What is the difference between extending and renewing a permit?

An extension is asked for before expiration. A renewal is asked for after. A void permit cannot be renewed; it needs a new permit.

How long do I have to revive an expired permit?

It depends on the city. Portland allows reactivation within 180 days. Maricopa County allows renewal within 360 days. Georgetown requires a new permit after 90 days.

How much does it cost to renew an expired building permit?

From a small fixed fee to half the original fee for a recent lapse, and full fees again for a long lapse. Pasco County charges $45 for an extension and $45 per open trade when only finals remain. Plano and Maricopa County charge half the original fee.

Can I keep working while my renewal is pending?

No. Until the permit is active again, the work has no live permit and can be treated as unpermitted work.

What if my expired permit is years old and the work is finished?

Ask about close-out. In Florida, a substantially completed expired permit can be closed without a new permit under the original code, and an agency may close a permit 6 years after issuance if no safety hazard is apparent.
