What building permits were issued in San Antonio and Bexar County this month?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 22, 2026 · County Briefs

QUICK ANSWER

San Antonio building permits issued in November 2026 so far total 3,124, counted from City of San Antonio records for Bexar County through November 20, the latest issue date in the feed. Plumbing leads at 929 permits, then electrical at 478 and general permits at 437. October closed at 5,363 permits, so November is on a normal pace for a short month that is not finished yet. Source: PermitsByCounty records, pulled November 22, 2026.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • 3,124 permits were issued in San Antonio and Bexar County from November 1 through November 20, 2026.
  • Plumbing is the busiest trade by a wide margin: 929 permits, about 3 in every 10 issued.
  • Roofing ran 245 permits and solar ran 28 in the same stretch — small counts, but both name a job a homeowner is paying for right now.
  • The busiest ZIP codes sit on the west and north sides: 78258, 78245, 78247, 78229 and 78249 lead the month.
  • Bexar County records carry counts, trades and ZIP codes well, but no contractor names and no usable dollar values — read this post as a count report, not a value report.

How to read this month’s numbers

This is a partial month, and that matters. The city feed reached November 20 when we pulled it on November 22, so the November total covers 20 days, not 30. October had a full month to reach 5,363. A straight side-by-side would make November look slow when it is mostly just unfinished.

A fairer read is the daily pace. October averaged about 173 permits a day. November 1 through 20 averaged about 156 a day. That is a little softer, which is normal heading into the holiday weeks, but it is not a drop-off. Work is still getting permitted across the county every day.

One more honesty note before the trade list. San Antonio’s permit register is a counts source for us. It does not publish a contractor or business name field, so we cannot tell you which company pulled a given permit from this feed, and most records do not carry a dollar value we can trust. Where a number below names a company or a price, it comes from a named outside source, not from the county record. What the record does give, cleanly, is what got permitted, which trade it belongs to, and where in the county it sits. For a sub or a supplier deciding where to spend a morning, that is the part that earns money anyway.

San Antonio building permits issued, by trade

Plumbing sits on top, and it is not close. The 929 plumbing permits in the first 20 days of November break down into 393 general plumbing permits, 250 irrigation permits, 212 gas permits, 141 LSR plumbing permits and 111 sewer permits, with the rest in smaller plumbing types. If you sell pipe, fixtures, water heaters or irrigation parts in Bexar County, this is your busiest shelf, month after month. October told the same story at 1,546 plumbing permits.

Electrical came second at 478, with 343 of them general electrical permits. HVAC, filed as mechanical permits, ran 355. General permits — the mixed repair and remodel bucket — ran 437, including 210 residential repair permits. Foundation repair permits ran 171, a steady San Antonio trade in a city where slab and soil problems are a plain fact of life.

Then come the trades that point at a single paid job. Re-roof permits ran 245. New residential building permits ran 86, with the new-construction trade count at 98 once related records are grouped. Sign permits ran 96. Fence permits ran 43, solar permits ran 28, and demolition permits ran 28.

Read the small numbers right. A solar permit is one homeowner spending real money this month, and the installer still needs racking, panels and an electrician. A demolition permit usually means something new is about to be built on that lot. Small counts in those trades are not weak signals. They are short lists you can actually work in a day.

A check against an outside count

You do not have to take our counting on faith for October. Texas Permit Watch, a site that publishes monthly San Antonio permit roundups by trade, counted 374 new roof permits and 64 solar permits in San Antonio for October 2026. Our own record count for October gives the same two numbers: 374 roofing permits and 64 solar permits. Two counters, working from the same city records, landing on the same totals is what a clean count looks like.

The same hunt that picked this post found Zabalist’s San Antonio permit search holding 137,921 issued permits in total. Our Bexar record holds 70,138 permits in the trailing window we track. Those totals differ because the two databases cover different spans of years, not because one of them is wrong. What matters for a working contractor is the current month, and there the counts line up.

Where the work is: the busiest ZIP codes

The month’s work leans west and north. ZIP code 78258, on the far north side, leads with 114 permits. Next door in spirit, 78245 on the far west side follows at 112, and 78247 in the north central area runs 106. The medical center area, 78229, counts 102. Then 78249 and 78201 sit at 97 each, 78230 at 94, and 78250 and 78223 at 88 each, with 78209 at 87.

That spread tells a supplier where a delivery route earns its fuel. The west and north edges are where the new building and the steady repair work stack up together. The inner ZIP codes, like 78201 and 78209, run fewer permits but a higher share of repair and remodel work, which suits trades that live on service calls rather than new installs.

October’s map rhymed with November’s. The same names sat at the top — 78247 at 220, then 78250, 78258, 78245 and 78223. When the same ZIP codes lead two months in a row, it stops being a blip and starts being a territory plan.

What this means if you sell time, crews or materials

If you run a trade crew, sort your week by the trade table, not by hope. Plumbing, electrical and HVAC together account for 1,762 of the month’s 3,124 permits — more than half of everything issued. Those three trades also pull each other along: a general repair permit today often turns into a plumbing or electrical call next week, because opening a wall tends to find more work.

If you supply materials, watch the permit types that eat product. Irrigation permits at 250 mean pipe, heads and controllers moving all month. Re-roof permits at 245 mean shingles, underlayment and tear-off work spread across the county, not stacked in one subdivision. Foundation permits at 171 mean piers, beams and concrete on a steady drip.

If you are a small sub looking for general contractors to work for, this feed will not hand you names — San Antonio’s register does not publish them, and any service that claims its Bexar list came from contractor names on these permits is selling you a different data source and should say so. Use this report for volume and place: which trades are busy, and in which ZIP codes. Then do your name-finding at the county report and trade pages, where the records that do carry business names live.

Paid services work this same ground. Builtie sells San Antonio leads timed to the permit stage, trade by trade, and Texas Permit Watch sells the roundup habit itself. The free version of the habit is simple: read the new permits on a fixed morning each week, note the trades and ZIP codes that keep repeating, and put your calls and your truck where the count already is.

Frequently asked questions

How many building permits were issued in San Antonio this month?

3,124 permits were issued in San Antonio and Bexar County from November 1 through November 20, 2026, the latest date in the city feed when we pulled it on November 22. October, a full month, closed at 5,363.

What is the busiest trade in San Antonio right now?

Plumbing, by a wide margin: 929 permits in the first 20 days of November 2026, counting general, irrigation, gas, LSR and sewer plumbing permits together. Electrical is second at 478, then general permits at 437 and HVAC at 355.

Which San Antonio ZIP codes have the most permits?

The far north and west sides lead in November 2026 so far: 78258 with 114 permits, 78245 with 112, 78247 with 106 and 78229 with 102. The same west and north ZIP codes led in October too.

Can I see which contractors pulled these permits?

Not from San Antonio’s register. The city source publishes no contractor or business name field, so PermitsByCounty reports Bexar County as counts, trades and ZIP codes only. Counties whose records do carry business names show them on their county report pages.

Why does November look smaller than October?

Because November is not finished. The feed reached November 20 at pull time, so the count covers 20 days against October’s full month. On a per-day basis November runs about 156 permits a day against October’s 173 — a little softer, not a slump.

How often is the San Antonio permit data updated?

The city file reloads on its own schedule and our nightly refresh checks it every night. This month’s report is based on the refresh of November 22, 2026, which added 1,146 net new Bexar permits and brought the latest issue date to November 20.
