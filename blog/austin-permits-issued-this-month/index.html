What building permits were issued in Austin this month?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published October 23, 2026 · County Briefs

QUICK ANSWER

Austin issued 3,137 building permits from October 1 through October 22, 2026 — the newest data in the City of Austin’s Issued Construction Permits record when we pulled it on October 23, 2026. Electrical led with 951, then plumbing with 770 and mechanical (HVAC) with 565. Most were residential: 2,289 against 848 commercial. October is not finished: September, the last full month, had 5,127. Source: City of Austin dataset, via the PermitsByCounty Travis County report, pulled October 23, 2026.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • Austin issued 3,137 permits through October 22, 2026 — a partial month, not a final total.
  • Electrical (951), plumbing (770) and HVAC (565) are more than two of every three permits this month.
  • Residential work is 2,289 of the 3,137 permits; commercial work is 848.
  • The busiest ZIP code is 78744 with 202 permits; the busiest named firm is Base Power with 145.
  • This post gives counts, not dollar totals, because Austin’s value field is not safe to add up — the reason is explained below.

First, what “this month” means here

“This month” means October 1 to October 22, 2026. That is simply how far the city’s record had reached when we pulled it: the dataset was last updated on October 22, 2026, and October 22 is the newest issue date in it. Permits issued later are not in this count yet; this brief will be refreshed with the next monthly update.

So read every number below as a partial month. The fair questions are not “is October down?” They are: what kind of work is being issued, where is it, and who is doing it? Those answers are already clear, and a subcontractor, a general contractor, or a supplier can act on them today.

One scope note, said plainly: this is the City of Austin’s permitting record, covering the city and its nearby permitting area. It is the record behind our Travis County report, and we say “Austin” where the data is Austin’s, because calling a city record a full county record would not be honest.

Which trades got the most permits this month?

The trade permits lead. A trade permit covers one kind of work — electrical, plumbing, or mechanical — and most building jobs need several of them. Each one is a job moving now, not a plan on a shelf.

Electrical came first with 951 permits, plumbing next with 770, and mechanical (heating and air) third with 565. Together they are 2,286 permits — about 73 of every 100 issued this month. After them: general building work 432, new construction 289, demolition 59, roofing 37, and solar 34. Those eight groups add up to the full 3,137.

Residential or commercial — which side is busier?

Residential, by a wide margin: 2,289 residential against 848 commercial this month — about 73 to 27. September had the same shape: 3,761 residential and 1,366 commercial out of 5,127.

The split matters because the two sides buy work differently. Residential trade permits are often pulled by the firm that will do the work, on homes already being built or fixed. Commercial permits are fewer, but each can hold a much bigger job. If you sell to home builders, the residential count is your weather report. If you sell to commercial builders, watch the new-construction list below.

How much new construction was permitted?

New construction — a permit for a new building or home, rather than a trade permit for one part of a job — came to 289 permits this month: 196 residential and 93 commercial. September, a full month, had 341.

October still has issuing days left and is already close to September’s total. New homes, garage apartments, pools, and commercial buildings are still starting at a steady pace. For suppliers, 289 starts in 22 days means 289 job sites that will soon need materials, site services, and trade help. The Travis County new-construction page lists this work month by month.

Which firms pulled the most permits this month?

Most permits name a contractor company — 2,857 of this month’s 3,137, about 91 in every 100. That makes a busiest-firms list possible, and it is one of the most useful lists here if you are looking for partners, builders to call, or competitors to watch.

Base Power pulled the most by a clear margin, with 145 permits. Next: Victory Plumbing Company 52, Texas Multifamily Solution, LLC 45, and IES Residential, Inc. 43. Two honest notes. First, the city prints names the way firms give them, so one business can appear under two close spellings — we keep the city’s spelling and do not merge names on a guess. Second, a long list does not always mean big jobs: many small trade permits can pass one large project. Use the list to choose who to call first.

Which parts of Austin are busiest this month?

South and central Austin lead. ZIP code 78744, in southeast Austin, has the most permits this month with 202. Next: 78704, just south of downtown, with 179; 78745 in south Austin with 170; 78757 in north-central Austin with 147; and 78747, also in the southeast, with 136. Work is spread across many areas, which is normal for trade permits — they follow homes and buildings all over town.

For a small firm, this is a simple planning tool: if your crew already works in 78744 or 78745, the work near you is thick this month. If you are weighing a new service area, start with the ZIP codes above and check the full list on the Travis County report.

How does this month compare with the months before it?

September, the last full month, had 5,127 permits. The months before it were in the same range: August 5,103, July 5,127, June 5,328, May 4,642, April 4,891, March 4,813. In plain words, a full Austin month in 2026 has meant roughly 4,600 to 5,300 issued permits.

October’s 3,137, with days still to count, sits in line with that pace. There is no drop and no boom to report — and we will not call either one from a partial month. The mix has stayed steady: electrical, plumbing, and HVAC on top, residential well ahead of commercial, new construction starting every week. When October closes, its final total joins the Travis County monthly archive.

Why does this post have no dollar total for Austin?

Because Austin’s value field cannot be added up honestly, and a wrong total is worse than no total. In the record we hold — 56,335 permits, October 17, 2025 to October 22, 2026 — most permits state no value or zero. Of those that state a value, most state exactly one dollar: a placeholder, not a price. And large projects state the same very large value on many permits at once, so adding the column would count one project many times over.

So we publish Austin counts, trades, areas, and firm names — the parts that check out — and no dollar totals, here or on the Travis County report. If the city fixes the field, we will check it again and say so.

How to read the Austin record yourself — and what to do with it

You can open the same record we used. The city publishes the dataset free on its open data portal, with permit type, issue date, work class, address area, and company name for each permit. It is a big, raw table — built for data work, not a quick phone read — which is why this brief exists: we count it, group it, and put the answer in plain English every month.

If you work in the trades, do three things with this brief. Find your trade and note its count — that is your market this month. Check the busiest ZIP codes against the areas you serve. Look at the busiest firms in your trade — their jobs are moving now. Then compare next month: one month is weather; a few months in a row is a pattern you can hire and buy against.

Frequently asked questions

What building permits were issued in Austin this month?

Austin issued 3,137 permits from October 1 through October 22, 2026, the newest data in the city’s record when we pulled it on October 23, 2026. Electrical led with 951, then plumbing with 770 and HVAC with 565. The month is not over, so the final October total will be higher.

Why is October’s total lower than September’s?

Because October is not finished. September’s 5,127 permits cover a full month; October’s 3,137 cover only October 1–22. Judged by its pace and its mix of work, October looks in line with the months before it — not a drop we can honestly claim from a partial count.

Which trade gets the most permits in Austin?

Electrical: 951 so far this month, and 16,184 in the past-year record we hold — ahead of plumbing (15,259) and HVAC (11,245). Trade permits lead because almost every building job needs electrical, plumbing, and air work.

Which Austin ZIP code has the most permits this month?

ZIP code 78744 in southeast Austin, with 202 permits from October 1–22, 2026. Next are 78704 with 179, 78745 with 170, 78757 with 147, and 78747 with 136.

Why don’t you publish the dollar value of Austin permits?

Because the city’s value field is not reliable enough to add up: most permits state no value or $0, most of the rest state exactly $1, and some large projects repeat the same full value on many permits. We publish the counts that check out and leave the dollar total out, rather than print a number that is not true.
