How many new homes are coming to the counties where I deliver?

By PermitsByCounty Research Team · Published November 13, 2026 · Guides & Answers

QUICK ANSWER

In October 2026, the counties we can count honestly issued 965 permits for new homes. That is the PermitsByCounty count, pulled November 12, 2026, from the permit records of 12 counties whose feeds were live and checked that morning. Los Angeles County issued the most, 307 — but 97 of those are wildfire rebuilds and 186 mention an ADU, so read that one with care. Next came Hillsborough County, Florida, at 193, and Travis County, Texas, at 176. A permit is not a finished home, and one permit can hold more than one home. Read the count as work on its way: each permit is a job site that will need materials delivered and trades on site in the months ahead.

TL;DR — the whole post in 20 seconds

  • 965 new-home permits were issued in October 2026 across the 12 counties we could count honestly.
  • Los Angeles leads at 307, but most of that number is fire rebuilds and ADUs — the biggest clean house counts are Hillsborough at 193 and Travis at 176.
  • Fort Worth, New York City, Chicago, and Orlando cannot be folded into that headline yet, each for a stated reason. Their honest numbers sit below, kept apart.
  • A permit is not a home and not a unit count. One townhome permit can hold 4 to 8 homes.
  • The county, the ZIP, and the builder name on each permit turn this count into a delivery list.

The count, county by county

Here is October 2026, county by county, in the order a supplier would read it — biggest pipeline first. Every count uses the same base rule: issued in October 2026, counted once, and counted only where the record itself says new home construction. What "says" means changes by county, because each county writes its records its own way. The counting note under each figure says which field carried the proof.

Los Angeles County, California, sits on top, and it needs the most care.

307 new residential building permits issued in October 2026; 97 of them mention wildfire or rebuild work and 186 mention an ADU; the top ZIP was 90272 (Pacific Palisades) with 97

So Los Angeles is not 307 new houses rising on empty lots. A large share is Pacific Palisades rebuilding after the fires, and ADUs — small second homes on lots that already have a house. For a supplier that is still real work: rebuilds and ADUs buy lumber, roofing, and fixtures too. Just do not plan a subdivision-sized delivery off this number.

Hillsborough County, Florida — the Tampa side — is the cleanest count in the set, because the county runs its own permit class for exactly this work.

193 permits issued in October 2026 under the county’s own Residential New Construction class, up from 181 in September; the top ZIP was 33572 with 34

Hillsborough County new homes — Hillsborough County, FL · October 2026

Source: PermitsByCounty Hillsborough County records · Date pulled: November 12, 2026

Travis County, Texas — Austin — comes next. We count building permits for a new dwelling and leave out additions, remodels, and pools. Austin’s permit values are not used in this post. Most of them read $0 or $1, so they say nothing.

176 new-home building permits issued in October 2026, up from 154 in September and 2,054 over the last 12 months; the top ZIP was 78704 with 21, and the builder named most often was Joseph Design Build with 17

Travis County new homes — Travis County (Austin), TX · October 2026

Wake County, North Carolina — the Raleigh side — shows why a permit count is a floor, not a home count. Many Wake townhome permits cover a whole building of 4 to 8 homes under one permit number.

95 new-home permits issued in October 2026; the top ZIP was 27616 with 32, and the builder named most often was Meritage Homes of the Carolinas with 22; the median value stated on the permits was $338,734

Wake County new homes — Wake County, NC · October 2026

That Wake figure also lines up with an outside read. TarSignal, which sells North Carolina permit data to subs and suppliers, counted 1,882 new single-family permits in Wake County’s office from January to September 2026, up 24 percent on the year before. Different counter, same direction: Wake is building.

Franklin County, Ohio — Columbus — is one of the few places that separates its housing types cleanly, so we can keep houses and apartment buildings apart instead of mixing them.

67 permits for new single-family and two-family houses issued in October 2026, plus 20 separate permits for buildings of three or more homes; the top ZIP was 43110 with 19, and the builder named most often was Maronda Homes with 19

Franklin County new homes — Franklin County (Columbus), OH · October 2026

Source: PermitsByCounty Franklin County records · Date pulled: November 12, 2026

Philadelphia issued 63 new-home building permits in October. We count the building permit only. The same project also draws a zoning permit, and counting both would count each home twice. Hamilton County, Ohio — Cincinnati — issued 20, with a median stated value of $140,000 on the permits. DeKalb County, Georgia, issued at least 23 that its records describe clearly enough to verify; many DeKalb records carry no description at all, so 23 is a floor, stated that way on purpose.

The small end of the list is honest too. Wayne County, Michigan — Detroit — issued 10 permits for new single-family and two-family homes, plus 12 permits for larger multi-family buildings, which we keep apart because some are big buildings, not houses. Allegheny County, Pennsylvania — Pittsburgh — issued 6. Cuyahoga County, Ohio — Cleveland — issued 5. San Francisco issued none that matched a new home in October; its feed runs only a few a month, and one September permit there was for a 233-unit tower — one permit, hundreds of homes, which is the clearest proof in this post that permits and homes are not the same unit.

Add the twelve up and you get the headline: 965 new-home permits in one month, across twelve counties, from records anyone can check.

How many new homes are coming where the headline cannot go?

Four places a supplier will ask about are not in the 965, and each one is out for a different reason. Leaving them out quietly would be worse than explaining.

Fort Worth is a feed problem, not a building problem. New-building permits in the Fort Worth feed stop after October 27, while remodel and solar permits keep flowing — the new-home records are lagging behind the rest. September is the last full month we trust there: 310 new-home permits, with ZIP 76179 on top at 67 and a median stated value of $390,300. Fort Worth was building at a Hillsborough-plus pace in September. We will not print an October number until the feed catches up.

New York City cannot be counted this way at all. The city files one permit per work type per job — general construction, foundation, plumbing, sprinklers, and more for a single building — so one new building can leave ten or so new-construction rows. A count of those rows would call one building ten buildings. Until we count city jobs address by address, New York stays out.

Chicago and Orlando sit one step closer. Chicago issued 165 new-construction permits of all kinds in October; at least 90 of them describe a home or dwelling units in their own text, but the records carry no clean residential split, and some of the 90 mix shops under homes. Orlando’s Orange County issued 61 residential new-construction permits in October, but its records name projects, not home types, and the set visibly holds apartment buildings. Both numbers are real. Neither one is a new-home count yet, so both stay beside the headline instead of inside it.

Two more feeds were set aside before counting started. The Miami-Dade and San Antonio (Bexar County) feeds came up stale in the November 12 check — their newest records were days old — so October counts from them would read low and lie. Harris County stays blocked, and the old Dallas open-data feed still ends in 2020. A county we cannot read fresh does not get a number.

What does a new-home permit mean for a delivery route?

A permit issued in October is not a delivery in October. Shovels, which sells permit data to building-material suppliers, puts the first gap plainly: building permits are filed 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks.

Building permits are filed 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks, and months before a project appears in industry reports

How early a permit signal is — United States · Supplier page checked November 12, 2026

Source: Shovels — Contractor Intelligence for Building Materials Suppliers · Date pulled: November 12, 2026

After ground breaks, the orders arrive in stage order — site and foundation materials first, framing next, then roofing, rough-in trades, and finishes near the end. A house takes months to build, so one October permit can mean deliveries spread across two or three seasons. Our guide to how suppliers quote a job before the material order is placed walks that stage order in full. For route planning, the short version is this: the October count is your winter and spring pipeline taking shape, not your October truck schedule.

Size the work from the permit, not from the count alone. A ZIP with 30 permits for one townhome project is one site and one buyer. Thirty permits scattered across a county are thirty sites, thirty stops, and often thirty different builders. The ZIP lines in the county figures above tell you which shape each county’s month took — 78704’s 21 permits in Austin are infill scattered through one close-in ZIP, while a top ZIP in a growth county usually means whole streets going up at once.

One national figure helps set the scale, as long as it stays in its place. The Census Bureau’s Building Permits Survey put new housing authorizations at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.40 million units in September 2026, 3.0 percent below September 2025. That survey counts new privately owned housing units only, and places that do not report get estimated. It can tell you the country slowed a touch. It cannot tell you that ZIP 33572 in Hillsborough issued 34 new-home permits last month, or who is building them. That is the gap a county count fills, and it is why we publish the county records themselves, addresses and builders included, instead of a national rate.

How do you turn this count into next month’s delivery list?

Start with the county you already serve, and pull its October list. Work it in four passes.

First pass: group by ZIP. Deliveries cluster where permits cluster. The top ZIP in each county above is where your truck will go most, and a ZIP that jumps month over month is a route asking to be added before a rival adds it.

Second pass: group by builder, where the records name one. Travis named a builder on 90 percent of its October new-home permits, Wake on 99 percent, Franklin on 97 percent, and Philadelphia on all of them. One call to the builder named on 22 Wake permits is worth more than 22 cold stops. Hillsborough, Los Angeles, Fort Worth, and Detroit publish no builder name on the permit, so in those counties the address and the ZIP carry the work instead.

Third pass: sort by stage. A permit issued in the last two weeks is site work and framing soon. A permit issued six weeks ago is moving toward roofing and rough-in. Match the load to the stage, and your delivery lands when the crew can use it.

Fourth pass: write down what you cannot see. Fort Worth’s October gap, New York’s uncountable rows, the stale feeds — a list that hides its blind spots sends a driver out short. A list that names them gets checked again next month, when the feeds have moved.

Frequently asked questions

How many new homes are coming to the counties where I deliver?

In October 2026, the 12 counties we could count honestly issued 965 new-home permits between them: Los Angeles 307 (mostly rebuilds and ADUs), Hillsborough 193, Travis 176, Wake 95, Franklin 67, Philadelphia 63, DeKalb at least 23, Hamilton 20, Wayne 10, Allegheny 6, Cuyahoga 5, and San Francisco 0. Fort Worth’s last trustworthy month, September, adds 310 more. Each permit is a home — or a small building of homes — on its way.

Is a permit the same as a home being built?

No. A permit is permission to build, issued before work starts — Shovels puts filing at 2 to 6 weeks before ground breaks. Some permitted homes are built late, changed, or not built at all. And one permit can cover several homes, like a Wake County townhome building of 4 to 8 units, so permits understate homes in one place and overstate starts in another. Treat the count as a pipeline, not a completion list.

Why is Fort Worth not in the October count?

Its new-building permits stop after October 27 in the feed while other permit types keep flowing, so an October total would be a part-month dressed as a month. September is complete: 310 new-home permits. We will add Fort Worth back the month its new-building records run to the end of the month.

Why can New York City not be counted the same way?

The city files one permit per work type per job, so a single new building can appear as ten or so new-construction records across general construction, foundation, plumbing, and other work types. Counting records would count buildings several times over. A New York count has to be built address by address, and we have not published one yet.

Which counties name the builder on a new-home permit?

In October 2026, Wake named a builder on 99 percent of its new-home permits, Franklin on 97 percent, Travis on 90 percent, and Philadelphia and Hamilton on all of theirs. Hillsborough, Los Angeles, Fort Worth, Wayne, and DeKalb publish no builder name on these permits, so there the address, ZIP, and project type are what you work from.

How is this different from the Census permit numbers?

The Census Building Permits Survey reports new privately owned housing units for the whole country — a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.40 million in September 2026 — and estimates the places that do not report. It carries no addresses and no builder names. The county counts here come from the permit records themselves, so you can see which ZIP, which street, and which builder each new home belongs to.
