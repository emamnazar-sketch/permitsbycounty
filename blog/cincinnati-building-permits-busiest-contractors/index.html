Read this first: what “Cincinnati” covers here

One honest note before the names. This list comes from City of Cincinnati permit records, which PermitsByCounty publishes under its Hamilton County report. Other towns in Hamilton County run their own permit offices and are not in these numbers. So read every count below as Cincinnati city work — that is where the register is strong, and it is the same coverage the county report page carries.

The counting rules are the ones this site always uses. Each permit counts once, by permit number. The month is the issue date on the record. A company’s name counts exactly as it is spelled on the permit, because that is what you will see when you look the record up yourself. When one firm shows up under two spellings, we say so out loud instead of folding them together in silence.

The busiest contractors in October 2026

Recker and Boerger sits on top with 30 permits. The split tells you what kind of shop it is: 22 of the 30 are plumbing permits and 8 are HVAC. That is a home-services firm running crews across the city every day, not a builder with one big project.

DMG Contractors Inc is second with 20 permits, and 18 of the 20 are HVAC. Apollo Heating & A/C is third with 19, all of them HVAC. Then the list spreads out: People Working Cooperatively and Kramer Plumbing Inc at 16 each, Schneller Heating & Air and Logan Services at 15 each, and Thomas & Galbraith at 14.

Three names sit at 13: Plumb Tech Services, Paradise Properties & Plumbing, and Otis Elevator Co. Otis is the one entry on this list that is not a local home-services shop — elevator work is permitted under its own trade, and a national firm holding 13 Cincinnati permits in a month says something real about how much vertical work the city has going.

Keep going down and the pattern holds. Zins Plumbing pulled 11. Romanoff Electrical, filed with a named electrician on the record, pulled 11 — the busiest electrical name in a register that carries no separate electrical trade count. Help Home Services pulled 13 under one spelling and 10 more under a near-identical one, Help Home Service Inc. Read together, that is 23 permits, which would put the firm second on this list. We keep the spellings apart in the table because the records do, but you should know the pair exists before you rank anyone.

Why heating and plumbing firms run this list

If you expected big builders at the top, the trade counts explain why they are not there. Plumbing is the largest trade in the October register at 303 permits. General permits — the mixed repair and remodel bucket — ran 225. HVAC ran 217. Fire protection ran 67, elevators 33, new construction just 22, signage 16 and demolition 10.

A furnace swap or a water heater job needs one permit each, and a busy shop files them week after week. A new building needs one permit too, and it can be worth more than a hundred service calls. Permit counts measure how often a firm shows up at the permit counter, not how much money it makes. For a sub or a supplier, that showing-up rate is the useful number anyway: it tells you whose phones ring, whose crews are out, and who is buying parts and materials on a steady drip.

The 12-month view backs that up. Over the full year in our records, Apollo Heating & A/C leads with 376 permits under that spelling, DMG Contractors follows at 342, and Recker and Boerger at 322. Add Apollo’s second spelling, Apollo Heating, Air Cond. & Plumbing, at 186, and the Apollo names together hold 562 permits across the year. The October leaders are not having one lucky month. They are the standing workload of this city.

November so far: the list is already shifting

October is the last full month, but November is not standing still. The feed carried 510 permits dated November 1 through November 19 when we pulled it. Recker and Boerger leads again, at 18. Help Home Services holds 14 under its main spelling. Then two names you did not see near the top in October: Kerr Excavating and Land Services at 12 and Sure Mechanical, filed with a named owner, at 10. Plumb Tech Services holds 11, and Onyx and East Ohio holds 10.

That churn is the point of watching this monthly instead of once. The top of the list is steady — the same home-services names month after month — but the next tier rotates, and the rotating names are often the firms adding crews or picking up a new line of work. Those are the doors worth knocking on first.

How to use a busiest list without wasting your week

Start with your own trade, not the headline names. If you do electrical work, the register will not hand you a clean electrical ranking — Cincinnati folds that work into other permit types, and the busiest electrical name in October filed 11 permits. Your better move is to read the general and HVAC lists and note which firms keep appearing, because those are the shops most likely to need a sub when a job grows.

If you sell materials or parts, sort by trade volume. Three hundred plumbing permits and 217 HVAC permits in one month is a standing order for pipe, fittings, units and filters. The firms at the top of this list buy every week, in small lots, all year. That is a different sale than chasing one big project, and for most suppliers it is the steadier one.

If you are a sub looking for general contractors, be honest about what this register can and cannot tell you. It names the company that pulled each permit, and in Cincinnati that is usually the trade firm doing the work, not a GC running the whole job. For GC hunting, the new-construction and general permit records are your slice — 22 and 225 permits in October. Small counts, but each one is a real project with a real address.

Two paid services sell this same ground in the Cincinnati metro. Cincinnati & Dayton Permit Reports charges $149 to $499 a month for permit reports here, and BuildIntel sells Cincinnati leads with builder rankings built in. Lakewave publishes its own Cincinnati rankings page. The free habit costs you one morning a month: read the new permits, note who keeps showing up, and put your calls where the count already is.

Frequently asked questions

Which contractor pulled the most permits in Cincinnati in October 2026?

Recker and Boerger, with 30 permits issued in October 2026 — 22 plumbing and 8 HVAC. DMG Contractors Inc was second with 20, and Apollo Heating & A/C third with 19.

Does this list cover all of Hamilton County?

No. It covers City of Cincinnati permit records, which PermitsByCounty publishes under its Hamilton County report. Other Hamilton County towns run their own permit offices, and their permits are not in these counts.

Why are the busiest firms HVAC and plumbing shops instead of builders?

Because this list counts permits, and service work files permits one job at a time, all month long. Plumbing was the largest trade in October 2026 at 303 permits and HVAC ran 217, while new construction ran 22. A builder can do a bigger job on a single permit.

Why does the same company appear under two names?

Because permit records carry the name as it was filed, and firms file under more than one spelling. Apollo Heating & A/C holds 19 permits in October 2026 and Apollo Heating, Air Cond. & Plumbing holds 9 more. We count spellings as filed and flag the pairs, so your own lookup matches ours.

How many permits did Cincinnati issue in October 2026?

893 permits, counted once by permit number and dated by issue date, naming 408 different companies. November 1 through 19 added 510 more.

How often does this ranking change?

The top names are steady across the year — Apollo Heating & A/C, DMG Contractors and Recker and Boerger lead the 12-month counts too — but the next tier rotates month to month, which is why the list is worth re-reading as each month closes.
